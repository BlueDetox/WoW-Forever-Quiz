    WarriorArms · Fury — 2 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Warrior — ArmsMeleeFirst-timer: MediumOne big two-handed weapon. Slow heavy hits + wounds that bleed over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Rend. Open with this — a wound that keeps bleeding over time.
    2. Heroic Strike. Your filler hit when you have spare rage (rage is the energy you build up by hitting enemies and being hit).
    3. Overpower. Use it whenever it lights up — it triggers when an enemy dodges you.
    4. Victory Rush. From level 20: use it right after a kill — it gives you health back.
    5. Mortal Strike. At level 40 this becomes your biggest hit. It also cuts the enemy's healing in half for a short time.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Battle Stance is your home while leveling — stay in it.
    • Sweeping Strikes (level 30) makes your hits splash onto a second nearby enemy, so two-enemy fights get much easier.

    Leveling tips

    • Your weapon IS your character: a better two-handed weapon is a bigger upgrade than any armor piece. Take weapon quest rewards first.
    • On gear, look for Strength first, then Stamina. Eat food between fights — you have almost no self-healing.

    Watch out

    • Pulling two or three enemies at once is how new warriors die. Pull one, finish it, eat, repeat.
    • Don't open every fight at full sprint — walking in with no rage means a slow start. It builds as the fight goes.
    Warrior — FuryMeleeFirst-timer: MediumTwo weapons, fast non-stop hits. Spin attack hits enemies around you.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Battle Shout. Keep this buff on yourself — it raises your attack power.
    2. Bloodthirst. Your main hit. Use it every time it's ready.
    3. Whirlwind. Spin and hit every enemy around you — great when two enemies are on you.
    4. Heroic Strike. Burns your extra rage on a harder hit.
    5. Execute. Only works on enemies under 20% health — it's your finisher. Save it for the end of a fight.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Two weapons means more hits, which means more rage — Fury fights speed up as they go.
    • Expect to miss more than an Arms warrior early on. Two-weapon fighting starts inaccurate and gets better with gear.

    Leveling tips

    • Keep BOTH weapons upgraded, not just one — a weak off-hand weapon drags the whole build down.
    • On gear: Strength and Agility. Agility also gives you critical hits, which Fury loves.

    Watch out

    • You still have no real self-healing. If a fight goes bad, run early — don't wait until you're almost dead.
    • Whirlwind feels great and pulls extra enemies by accident. Check what's standing near your target before you spin.
    PaladinRetribution — 1 damage option, tap to open⌄
    Paladin — RetributionMeleeFirst-timer: EasyHoly knight, big weapon. Can heal itself + a bubble makes it immune briefly.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Keep a Seal active. A Seal adds holy damage to every swing. Once you learn Seal of Command, that's your main one.
    2. Holy Strike. From level 6 — a holy melee hit. One of your first real buttons.
    3. Judgement. Spends your Seal's power on a burst of holy damage. In WoW Forever your Seal stays up afterwards, so judge freely.
    4. Consecration. From level 20 — lays holy fire on the ground. Use it when enemies stand grouped together.
    5. Hammer of Wrath. A ranged holy hammer that only works on enemies under 20% health — your finisher.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Seal of Fury (level 10) adds holy damage to your swings and gives you a small damage shield on top.
    • Your bubble (Divine Shield) makes you immune for a few seconds. It's your panic button — hit it, heal, then keep fighting.
    • You can heal yourself with your holy spells between fights. That's your big solo advantage over a warrior.

    Leveling tips

    • Use a slow, heavy two-handed weapon — Seal of Command hits much harder with slow weapons.
    • On gear: Strength and Stamina first, with some Intellect so you have mana to heal.

    Watch out

    • You have no interrupt and no slow: enemy casters will finish their spells, and runners will run. Finish runners with Hammer of Wrath or Judgement.
    • Don't spend all your mana healing in the middle of a fight — an empty paladin can't fight back. Heal between fights instead.
    HunterBeast Mastery · Marksmanship · Survival — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Hunter — Beast MasteryRanged + PetFirst-timer: EasyYour pet takes the hits for you while you shoot from far away. Easiest alone.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Hunter's Mark. Put this on the enemy first — it takes extra damage from you.
    2. Send your pet in. Your pet attacks first and uses Growl, which forces the enemy to fight the pet instead of you.
    3. Serpent Sting. A poison shot that keeps hurting over time. Put it on and leave it.
    4. Arcane Shot. Instant damage, no waiting. Your main shot while leveling.
    5. Bestial Wrath. A talent later in the tree: your pet rages for 18 seconds, dealing 50% more damage and impossible to stop. Save it for hard fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • You tame your first real pet at level 10 — and in WoW Forever you can tame beasts up to 2 levels higher than you. Visit a pet trainer to teach it Growl and other skills.
    • Your pet gets stronger from YOUR gear, so your upgrades are its upgrades too.
    • At level 20 all hunters learn Aimed Shot. It shares a cooldown with Multi-Shot, so pick one per situation.
    • If you take the Summon Hawk talent, it shares a cooldown with Arcane Shot — one or the other each time.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility above everything — it powers you and your pet.
    • Traps work during combat in WoW Forever. A Freezing Trap can park one extra enemy while your pet handles another.

    Watch out

    • If your pet dies, everything runs straight at a lightly-armored you. Heal it with Mend Pet during hard fights, and let it get the enemy's attention before you start shooting.
    Hunter — MarksmanshipRangedFirst-timer: EasyA sniper. Slow, big shots from far away. Can play with no pet for +20% damage.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Hunter's Mark. Always first — marked enemies take more damage from you.
    2. Aimed Shot. From level 20: your huge shot. It takes 2 seconds to fire and you must stand still — open fights with it.
    3. Arcane Shot. Instant filler damage between bigger shots.
    4. Multi-Shot. Hits up to 3 nearby enemies. It shares a cooldown with Aimed Shot: Multi-Shot for groups, Aimed Shot for one target.
    5. Auto Shot. Your bow or gun keeps firing on its own — as long as you keep your distance and give it a moment standing still to fire.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • The Lone Wolf talent gives you +20% damage with NO pet at all. It's strong, but nothing holds enemies off you — only take it once keeping your distance feels easy.
    • Sniper Shot, at 31 points in the tree, is this spec's long-range finisher talent.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first. A slower bow or gun with bigger damage numbers usually beats a fast, weak one.
    • Fight at maximum range. Distance is your armor.

    Watch out

    • In WoW Forever your Auto Shot needs a brief stand-still before it fires — constant stutter-stepping quietly kills your damage. Plant your feet, shoot, then move.
    • If an enemy reaches you, your shots get interrupted. Slow it (Concussive Shot), back up, and reset your range.
    Hunter — SurvivalMelee + PetFirst-timer: MediumFights up close next to its pet. Sets traps that hold or hurt enemies.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Open from range. Start with your shots the way every hunter does — then stay in close and keep fighting.
    2. Raptor Strike. A hard melee hit. One of your main close-range buttons.
    3. Mongoose Bite. A counter-hit that becomes available in melee. With the Lacerating Strikes talent, it also makes the enemy bleed.
    4. Wing Clip. Slows an enemy's movement — use it on anything trying to run, or to buy yourself space.
    5. Your traps. Freezing Trap parks an enemy in ice; Immolation Trap sets one on fire. In WoW Forever traps work in combat, and they're real weapons for you.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Expose Prey (talent) can set up Mongoose Bite without waiting for a dodge — the tree is built around that combo.
    • You're the only hunter who wants to be in melee. Your pet still matters: it holds one enemy while you duel another.

    Leveling tips

    • You need TWO good weapons: your bow/gun and your melee weapon. Don't let either one fall behind.
    • On gear: Agility and Stamina — you take more hits than other hunters.

    Watch out

    • You wear hunter armor, not warrior armor. Dueling two enemies at once in melee is a loss — trap one first, then fight the other.
    RogueAssassination · Combat · Subtlety — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Rogue — AssassinationMeleeFirst-timer: MediumStabs with daggers. Poisons keep damaging the enemy over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Stealth opener. Start fights hidden. Ambush for a huge first hit, or Garrote to start a bleed.
    2. Sinister Strike. Your builder: each hit adds a combo point on the enemy (up to 5).
    3. Keep poisons on. Coat your weapons with poison and re-apply it when it wears off — a steady share of your damage comes from poison.
    4. Slice and Dice. Spend a couple of combo points on this early — you attack faster while it's running.
    5. Eviscerate. Spend 4–5 combo points on this finisher for your biggest hit.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Combo points are the rogue engine: builders add points, finishers spend them, and more points means a stronger finisher.
    • Your energy bar refills on its own over a few seconds. Starting a fight at full energy matters more than any single button.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first, then Strength. Keep your main-hand weapon sharp and current — most of your damage starts there.
    • Sap one enemy before fighting a group — it sleeps through the whole fight if nobody hits it.

    Watch out

    • You have no self-healing at all. Eat between fights, and if a pull goes wrong, Vanish and walk away instead of dying bravely.
    Rogue — CombatMeleeFirst-timer: MediumMore head-on than other rogues, less sneaking. Steady sword / axe attacks.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Sinister Strike. Your builder — adds combo points. This is most of your button presses.
    2. Slice and Dice. Spend your first few combo points here: attacking faster means more of everything.
    3. Eviscerate. Your finisher at 4–5 combo points.
    4. Blade Flurry. For two-enemy fights: your attacks also hit a second nearby enemy.
    5. Adrenaline Rush. The tree's big talent: your energy floods back for a short burst window. Save it for hard fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever, poisons and sharpening stones work TOGETHER instead of replacing each other — use both.
    • One-handed axes are rogue weapons here too, so swords, maces and axes are all fair game.
    • You stay in Stealth after using Sap in WoW Forever — sap, then pick your real target.

    Leveling tips

    • A slow main-hand weapon makes each Sinister Strike hit harder. On gear: Agility and Strength.
    • This is the head-on rogue — of the three, it spends the least time worrying about stealth.

    Watch out

    • Melee only and no self-heals, so eat between pulls. Two enemies at once without Blade Flurry ready is a bad trade.
    Rogue — SubtletyMeleeFirst-timer: HarderSneaks unseen and strikes from hiding. Wounds keep bleeding over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Stealth opener. Cheap Shot stuns the enemy as you open; Ambush is the huge-damage option with a dagger. Pick per fight.
    2. Hemorrhage. Your main attack out in the open — it wounds the enemy so it keeps suffering from your hits.
    3. Backstab. Only works from behind the enemy — and it's one of your hardest hits. Get behind whenever you can.
    4. Kidney Shot. Spends combo points to stun. Use it to stop a caster or to breathe mid-fight.
    5. Eviscerate. Your damage finisher at 4–5 combo points.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Preparation (talent) instantly refreshes your other cooldowns — that's a second Vanish and a second Blind in one fight. It's your reset button.
    • Elusiveness (talent) cuts the cooldowns of Vanish and Blind, so your escapes come back faster than other rogues'.

    Leveling tips

    • Positioning is damage: behind the enemy is where this spec lives. Circle as you fight.
    • Daggers make your stealth openers and Backstab hit their hardest. On gear: Agility first.

    Watch out

    • Your whole plan starts in stealth. If an enemy sees you first, the opener is gone and the fight is twice as hard — approach pulls slowly and from the side.
    PriestShadow — 1 damage option, tap to open⌄
    Priest — ShadowRangedFirst-timer: MediumDark spells hurt over time. Shadow form boosts them, damage heals group a bit.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Shadow Word: Pain. Always first — shadow damage that keeps ticking over time.
    2. Mind Blast. Your direct shadow hit. Use it whenever it's ready.
    3. Mind Flay. A channeled shadow beam that also slows the enemy's movement — good while it walks toward you.
    4. Shoot (your wand). Between spells, your wand does real damage. In WoW Forever you need to stand still for wand shots to fire.
    5. Shadow Word: Death. From level 32: a heavy finisher — use it to end fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Shadowform, the tree's form, makes your shadow spells cheaper in WoW Forever, so long fights strain your mana less.
    • Your damage trickle-heals your group a little while you fight (that's from your shadow talents) — in a group, you quietly patch everyone.
    • At level 20 every priest gets Devouring Plague (another damage-over-time spell) and Fear Ward (protection against being feared).
    • The Spirit Tap talent gives you mana back after kills — it's what keeps shadow leveling moving.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. And treat your wand like a real weapon — upgrade it whenever you can.
    • Shield yourself with Power Word: Shield BEFORE the pull starts, not in a panic during it.

    Watch out

    • You wear cloth — the lightest armor in the game. If two enemies reach you, you're in trouble: fear one away (Psychic Scream) and finish the other.
    ShamanElemental · Enhancement — 2 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Shaman — ElementalRangedFirst-timer: EasyLightning from far away + fire that keeps burning. Totems make your group stronger.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Flame Shock. Sets the enemy burning over time. Open with it.
    2. Lightning Bolt. Your main cast — most of your damage comes from this.
    3. Chain Lightning. When 2 or more enemies stand together, this jumps between them.
    4. Earth Shock. Instant damage — and it interrupts an enemy's spell cast. Save it for when you see an enemy casting something nasty.
    5. Lightning Shield. Keep this buff on yourself: attackers get zapped when they hit you.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • There's no Lava Burst in WoW Forever — this tree's big talent is Call of Thunder, which supercharges your lightning instead.
    • Totems are placeable helpers that last a short while. Searing Totem adds fire damage; others strengthen your whole group. Drop them when a fight will last, not for a 5-second kill.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Drink between pulls to refill mana.
    • You wear mail armor — tougher than cloth casters — but you're still a caster. Keep your distance.

    Watch out

    • Getting hit pushes your casts back and slows them down. If an enemy reaches you, Frost Shock slows it — step away, then cast.
    Shaman — EnhancementMeleeFirst-timer: MediumWeapon can hit extra times for free. Melee hits power up instant lightning.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Keep your weapon imbue up. Rockbiter Weapon early on; from level 30, Windfury Weapon — the one that makes your weapon hit extra times for free.
    2. Stormstrike. Once you learn it, this is your biggest weapon hit. Use it on cooldown.
    3. Flame Shock. A burn over time — cheap and always worth having on the enemy.
    4. Lightning Bolt. Your melee hits build up a talent (Maelstrom Weapon) that makes Lightning Bolt instant. When it's ready, fire it off — free damage.
    5. Earth Shock. Instant damage that also interrupts enemy spell casts. Shocks share one cooldown, so pick the right shock for the moment.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever your weapon imbues work alongside sharpening stones — use both.
    • Totem presets (level 20) let you drop your usual totems much faster.
    • Two-handed axes and maces are yours by default here — a slow two-hander with Windfury is the classic leveling route.

    Leveling tips

    • A slow weapon wins with Windfury: fewer, bigger swings mean bigger free extra hits. On gear: Strength and Agility.
    • Your melee hits power your spells and your spells support your melee — if you're only doing one half, you're playing half the spec.

    Watch out

    • Healing mid-fight empties the mana you need for shocks and costs you damage. Top yourself up AFTER the fight, and keep a totem down when fights will last.
    MageArcane · Fire · Frost — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Mage — ArcaneRangedFirst-timer: MediumEach magic blast makes you stronger, but costs more mana (your spell energy).Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Arcane Blast. Your main nuke. Each cast makes the NEXT Arcane Blast stronger — but also costs more mana. That's the whole spec in one button.
    2. Arcane Missiles. A channeled stream of missiles. Steady, reliable damage when you need to mind your mana.
    3. Frostbolt. A cheap, safe filler — and it slows the enemy walking at you.
    4. Evocation. Channel to refill a big chunk of mana. Use it between fights or when you're safely out of reach.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Mana is your real health bar. Blast stacks feel amazing and drain you in seconds — the skill is knowing when to stop stacking and coast.
    • Conjure your own water and drink often. Running dry mid-fight means standing there doing nothing.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect above all — a bigger mana pool means more Arcane Blasts before you're empty.
    • A good rhythm while learning: a few Arcane Blasts, then Missiles until your mana looks healthy again.

    Watch out

    • Going all-out on every normal enemy leaves you drinking after every fight — or dead when an extra enemy joins. Pace the blasts: small fights get small spending.
    Mage — FireRangedFirst-timer: MediumCrits (extra-strong hits) speed up your next huge fireball, Pyroblast.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Fireball. Your main cast — big, slow, dependable fire damage.
    2. Scorch. A fast, cheap fire cast. Good for finishing enemies and while you're learning.
    3. Fire Blast. Instant — no cast time. Use it while moving or to finish something now.
    4. Pyroblast. Your huge fireball. Critical hits build Heating Up, which stacks and makes Pyroblast cast faster and faster — launch it when it's charged up.
    5. Flamestrike. For big groups: fire rains on an area. Only worth it on 3+ enemies.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Crits are your engine: every critical hit feeds Heating Up, and Heating Up feeds Pyroblast. The spec snowballs — the more crit you have, the more often the big one flies.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect first, and take critical-hit gear when you see it — it literally makes your best button happen more.
    • Drink between fights. Fire is thirsty.

    Watch out

    • Long cast times plus cloth armor: if an enemy reaches you, stop casting, Frost Nova it in place (yes, fire mages have Frost Nova), step back, and restart.
    Mage — FrostRangedFirst-timer: EasyFreezes enemies in place + slows them. Safest mage, good against groups.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Frostbolt. Your main cast — and it slows the enemy, so they take longer to reach you.
    2. Frost Nova. Freezes enemies around you in place. Cast it, then STEP BACK before you keep casting.
    3. Cone of Cold. Instant frost in a cone in front of you — good damage and a strong slow.
    4. Blizzard. For groups: ice rains on an area and slows everything in it (40% with the Improved Blizzard talent).
    5. Fire Blast. Instant finisher for enemies that almost died — every mage has it, frost included.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Fingers of Frost (talent): frozen enemies give your spells a big crit chance — when something is frozen, hit it with your hardest spell.
    • Freezes can break early when the frozen enemy takes damage, and bosses can't be frozen at all — in boss fights, your slows and Fingers of Frost do the work instead.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Polymorph is your best friend on multi-enemy pulls: turn one enemy into a sheep, fight the others, come back for it.
    • This is the safest mage, not the fastest — fights take a little longer and you almost never die. That's the trade, and it's a good one for a first character.

    Watch out

    • Don't stand still in melee range trading hits — your whole kit is built on distance. If something reaches you: Frost Nova, step back, keep casting.

    Warlock — AfflictionRangedFirst-timer: EasyLoads enemies with many slow damage spells. Their health drains over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Agony. Your strongest damage-over-time spell — it grows stronger the longer it stays on. In WoW Forever Agony is a 'Bane', which means it no longer blocks your Curses: you can run a Bane AND a Curse at the same time.
    2. Corruption. A second damage-over-time spell. Load it on right after Agony.
    3. Shadow Bolt. Your filler while the drains do their work.
    4. Drain Life. Channels health out of the enemy and into you — damage and healing in one button.
    5. Siphon Life. A talent: another drain that quietly heals you every few seconds while it ticks.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever your damage-over-time spells can land critical hits — your drains hit harder than classic players remember.
    • Wrack is this tree's key talent, built around your Agony. The whole spec is: load drains, keep them rolling, stay alive.
    • Life Tap turns your health into mana, and your drains turn it back. That loop is why this warlock almost never has to stop.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Stamina — stamina is fuel for Life Tap, not just health.
    • Fear is your crowd control: one enemy runs away scared while you finish another. Practice it before you need it.

    Watch out

    • Drains kill slowly at the start of a fight. Don't pull three enemies at once until your drains are strong — one extra pull is how affliction warlocks die.

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Send your demon in first. Your Voidwalker holds the enemy's attention. Let it arrive before you cast anything.
    2. Corruption and Agony. Load your damage-over-time spells once the demon has the enemy.
    3. Shadow Bolt. Your filler damage between refreshes.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Stamina and Intellect. Your demon scales with your stats, so your gear is its gear.
    • Keep Soul Shards stocked — summoning demons costs them.

    Watch out

    • Your demon is tough, not immortal — pet durability was specifically tuned at launch. If it dies, everything turns on you: Fear the enemy, resummon, or run.

    • You can leave form and heal yourself at any time. That self-heal is the druid advantage: slower fights, almost no deaths.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Root-and-cast is your safest pattern: root, back up, Starfire.
    • Carry drinks — Balance is mana-hungry, and shapeshifting itself costs mana too.

    Watch out

    • If a fight goes wrong, don't die in owl form: shift to Bear Form for the armor, or Travel/Cat speed to run, THEN recover. New balance druids forget their forms are escape buttons.

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Prowl, then open. Your stealth. Pounce stuns the enemy as you open; Ravage is the big-damage opener. Pick per fight.
    2. Claw. Your builder in cat form: spends energy, adds a combo point (up to 5, shown on the enemy).
    3. Shred. Your hardest builder — but only from BEHIND the enemy. Get behind whenever you can.
    4. Rip. Spend combo points on this finisher: a bleed that ticks over time. More points = a longer bleed. This is your best damage per energy.
    5. Ferocious Bite. The other finisher: instant burst. Use it when Rip is already ticking or to finish an enemy off.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Cat form runs on energy and combo points, like a rogue — builders add points, finishers spend them, and waiting a beat for energy is normal, not a mistake.
    • Bear Form is your emergency button: huge armor and health. Frenzied Regeneration in bear form can turn rage into a massive heal.
    • Leader of the Pack (talent) raises your whole group's critical-hit chance — in a group, people are glad you're there.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first — it powers cat damage and your dodging in bear too.
    • Bleed first, bite second: a fight that opens Rip → Shred → Ferocious Bite beats button-mashing every time.

    Watch out

    • Dumping all your energy the second you have it leaves you standing there auto-attacking. Pool your energy, spend it in bursts — and if you pull too much, Bear Form and run.

    Play togetherTap to open⌄

    WoW Forever has no realm list — you pick a ruleset (start on Normal), and everyone on that ruleset shares one world. To group up you must be on the same ruleset AND the same faction (Alliance or Horde) as your friends. Decide together before anyone creates a character.

    Making your first characterTap to open⌄

    Your race decides your faction. Alliance races: Human, Dwarf, Night Elf, Gnome. Horde races: Orc, Troll, Tauren, Undead (a human who died and came back — they're on the Horde side here). You can only group with your own faction, so this is the choice to make together.

    Race is the small choice: each race gets a couple of minor special abilities, and that's mostly it. Class is the big choice — it decides how you fight for the entire game. If you're stuck between two races, pick the one you like looking at. You'll be staring at this character for a long time.

    Your name has to be unique on your ruleset. Pick something you won't be embarrassed to see in chat for the next year.

    Playing together from level 1Tap to open⌄

    Invite your friends into a party (a group of up to 5). Party members who are near each other share credit for quest kills — if your friend kills the wolf the quest sent you after, it counts for you too.

    Gathering quests are the exception: if the quest wants 8 wolf pelts, each of you needs your own 8 drops. Expect gathering quests to take the group a little longer — that's normal, not a bug.

    Each race starts in its own starting zone, so a Human and a Night Elf begin on opposite sides of the world. Don't panic: by around level 10–12 the starting zones feed into shared areas, and meeting up gets easy. Until then, everyone levels in their own zone.

    You can share most quests from your quest log with party members who are eligible — when you pick up a new quest, share it before you set off.

    Your characterTalents start at level 10 · Stats in plain language · Gear — colors, weapons, and armor · Trainers — your abilities aren't automatic — tap to open⌄
    Talents start at level 10Tap to open⌄

    You earn talent points as you level — 51 by level 60 — and spend them in your class's three trees. Your 'spec' is simply the tree you spend the most in. A class trainer in any major city can reset them, and at level 40 you unlock a second talent set you can swap to.

    Stats in plain languageTap to open⌄

    Strength — makes melee hits harder. The main stat for warriors and paladins, useful for anyone who swings a weapon.

    Agility — faster critical hits (extra-strong hits), more dodging, a little armor. The main stat for hunters and rogues, and for druids in cat form.

    Intellect — a bigger mana pool (mana is your spell energy), and your spells land critical hits slightly more often. The main stat for mages, priests, and warlocks.

    Spirit — faster health and mana regeneration when you're NOT in combat. It shortens your eating and drinking breaks, which quietly speeds up leveling.

    Stamina — more health. Everyone wants some; it's what keeps a bad pull from killing you.

    You don't need to memorize this for every class: each starter guide names the two stats that matter for that character. Gear whose main stat isn't yours is sell-it gear, no matter how shiny it looks.

    Gear — colors, weapons, and armorTap to open⌄

    Item names come in colors. Grey = junk, sell it to any vendor. White = plain but usable. Green = has bonus stats, your normal leveling gear. Blue = rare, a real upgrade. Purple = epic, the best tier — you won't see much of it while leveling, and that's fine.

    Two words on gear matter. 'Binds on pickup' means once you pick it up, it's yours forever — you can never trade or sell it to another player. 'Binds on equip' means you can trade or sell it until the moment you wear it.

    For melee classes and hunters, a better weapon is a bigger upgrade than any armor piece — your damage numbers come straight from it. Take weapon quest rewards first.

    Armor comes in four weights: cloth (lightest), leather, mail, plate (heaviest). Wear the heaviest type your class can use — warriors and paladins start in mail, hunters and shamans in leather, and at level 40 those classes can train the next weight up.

    Trainers — your abilities aren't automaticTap to open⌄

    Leveling up does NOT hand you your new spells. Every couple of levels, visit your class trainer in a town or capital city and BUY your new abilities — and the stronger ranks of the ones you already have. Forgetting this is the #1 reason new players feel mysteriously weak at level 15.

    The same trainer can reset your talent points if you want to change your build. It costs gold, and the price goes up each time you do it, so experiment early while it's cheap.

    Professions have trainers too — you learn each job, and its recipes and patterns, from them. Your class trainer is usually near other trainers in the same city district.

    Stopping enemy spells — interruptsTap to open⌄

    Casting and cast bars. When an enemy uses a big spell, a bar appears and fills up. If it finishes, the spell happens. While it fills, the enemy can be stopped.

    Interrupt. An ability that cancels the spell being cast AND locks that kind of spell for a few seconds. Yours: Rogue — Kick. Warrior — Pummel. Mage — Counterspell. Shaman — Earth Shock. A warlock's Felhunter pet has one too (Spell Lock). Shadow priests can take the Silence talent. Hunters, druids and paladins have NO true interrupt — read the next line.

    Stuns and silences. The backup plan: a stunned or silenced enemy can't cast either. Paladin — Hammer of Justice. Rogue — Cheap Shot / Kidney Shot. Druid — Bash (in Bear Form). Hunter — Intimidation (Beast Mastery pet stun) or Scatter Shot (Marksmanship). If your class can't interrupt, stunning and killing the caster fast is your job.

    What to interrupt first. Enemy HEALS — an interrupted heal is a dead enemy. Then fears and mind controls (they turn your own side against you), then big damage spells aimed at your tank. A normal small spell usually isn't worth it.

    If nobody interrupts. Healers heal forever, summoners summon friends, and fear casters scatter your group. In dungeons, 'we died because nothing got interrupted' is one of the most common wipe causes there is.

    Aggro — who the enemy attacksTap to open⌄

    Threat (aggro). Every enemy keeps a mental list of who has annoyed it most — damage and healing both count. Whoever is on top gets attacked. 'Having aggro' means it's coming for you.

    The tank's job. Staying on top of that list on purpose, with armor and a shield to survive it. One player tanks, everyone else's job includes NOT climbing above them.

    Taunt. The tank's rescue button: forces an enemy to attack the tank for a few seconds, no matter what the list said. If something runs past the tank at your healer, the tank taunts it back.

    Your rules as damage. Let the tank hit the enemy a few times BEFORE you go all-out. Attack the same enemy the tank is attacking. If an enemy does chase you, run TOWARD the tank (so they can take it off you) — never away, and never keep hitting it.

    Pet tanks. Same rules with a pet: let your pet hit first and build its lead, then shoot. If you open with your biggest shot, the enemy skips the pet and comes straight for you.

    Crowd control — taking enemies out of the fightTap to open⌄

    Crowd control (CC). Abilities that disable an enemy without killing it, so your group fights one enemy at a time instead of four: a mage's Polymorph turns one into a sheep, a rogue's Sap knocks one out before the fight, a hunter's Freezing Trap freezes one, roots pin one in place, fear sends one running.

    CC breaks. Sheep, traps and Sap BREAK the moment that enemy takes damage. One careless area spell can wake everything up at once. Never hit a disabled enemy, and don't use area attacks near one.

    Target marks. Group leaders stick icons on enemies. Skull almost always means 'kill this one first'. Moon usually means 'mage, sheep this one'. If you're not sure what a mark means in your group, ask — it's a normal question.

    Focus fire. The flip side of CC: everyone attacks the SAME awake enemy until it's dead. Five half-dead enemies hit just as hard as five healthy ones — kills shrink the danger, damage spread everywhere doesn't.

    Your other buttons — staying aliveTap to open⌄

    Defensive cooldowns. Every class has one or two 'oh no' buttons (a paladin's bubble, a mage's Ice Block, a rogue's Vanish, a warrior's Shield Wall). Use them when a fight suddenly goes wrong or you're near 30% health. A defensive you never press saves nobody — they recharge.

    Potions and bandages. Health potions are instant and work mid-fight. Bandages (from the First Aid skill) heal a lot but break if you're hit — bandage when nothing is attacking you. Carry both; vendors and other players sell them.

    Cooldowns. Your strongest abilities can't be spammed — they recharge over seconds or minutes (that's the 'cooldown'). Big cooldowns are for big moments: hard enemies, extra pulls, bosses. Using one on a normal enemy then facing a boss without it is the classic trade-off.

    Food, drink and buffs. Eat and drink between fights — full health and mana, faster starts. Some food and spells give 'buffs': temporary stat boosts. In a group, share yours; someone will share theirs.

    Where you stand — positioningTap to open⌄

    Don't stand in it. Fire on the ground, poison pools, glowing circles: if it's under your feet and you didn't put it there, move. Most avoidable deaths in the game are just standing still in something bad.

    Front and back. Enemies mostly hit what's in front of them. Rogues and cat druids literally do more damage from BEHIND. When someone else has the enemy's attention, stand behind it — safer and stronger.

    Line of sight. Enemy casters can't cast through walls. Pull them around a corner and they have to walk to you — straight into your group. Tanks do this on purpose; don't stand out in the open undoing it.

    Runners. Many enemies try to flee at low health — and they run TO their friends, bringing them back. Slow them, stun them, or finish them fast. A runner nobody stopped is how a 2-enemy fight becomes a 6-enemy fight.

    Words you'll hear — quick translationsTap to open⌄

    Mob. Any computer-controlled enemy. (From old game slang — it's just 'a monster'.)

    Pull. Starting a fight with an enemy or group, usually one pack at a time.

    Add. An extra enemy that joins a fight already in progress. Usually bad news.

    Elite. A much stronger enemy with a gold dragon around its portrait. Elites are built for groups — solo players should walk around them.

    Buff / debuff. A helpful temporary effect on you (buff) or a harmful one on you or an enemy (debuff). Curses, poisons and diseases are debuffs some classes can remove ('dispel' / 'cleanse').

    DoT / HoT. Damage over Time / Healing over Time — effects that keep ticking after one button press.

    Proc. A random bonus effect triggering — 'it procced' means the lucky thing happened.

    Crit. A critical hit: an extra-strong hit (usually double damage) that happens by chance.

    Spec. Your specialization — the talent tree you put most points in. 'What's your spec?' means 'which of the three versions of your class are you?'

    Wipe. The whole group died. Everyone runs back together and tries again — normal, not a disaster.

    NPC. Any computer-controlled character — quest givers, vendors, trainers. Yellow name = neutral, red = will attack, green = friendly.

    Out in the world — Forever systemsTap to open⌄

    Hearthstone. A stone in your bag that teleports you back to one chosen inn (10-second cast, 1-hour cooldown). Talk to the innkeeper in each new quest town and set it there — it's your 'go home' button.

    Rested experience. Logging out inside an inn or a capital city builds up rested XP (a little every few hours, up to about a level and a half). While you have it, kills give DOUBLE experience. Log out in inns — it's free progress while you sleep.

    Campfires. A new WoW Forever system: around level 5 a quest teaches you Basic Campfire (from Cooking). Camps and campfires out in the world give rested XP and useful buffs, and other players can add to your fire. Worth learning early.

    Dying. You turn into a ghost and run back to your body to revive where you fell. A Spirit Healer can revive you instantly instead, but it damages your gear and — above level 10 — leaves you weak (resurrection sickness) for up to 10 minutes. Running back is almost always better.

    Repairs. Your gear wears out as you fight and especially when you die. Vendors repair it for coins — repair before dungeon runs, or your armor quietly breaks mid-fight.

    Trainers. New abilities aren't automatic: every couple of levels, visit your class trainer in a town or city and BUY your new spells and their stronger ranks. Forgetting this is why some new players feel mysteriously weak.

    Getting a group. There's no automatic dungeon queue in WoW Forever: groups form through the listing tool and chat, and you walk to the dungeon entrance together. Slow, but it's how you'll meet people.

    Your first mount. At level 40 you can learn riding and buy your first mount (about 100 gold) — much faster travel. It's the big purchase of your leveling life: don't blow all your gold at level 30.

    The leveling loopTap to open⌄

    Quest, kill one enemy at a time at first, loot everything, and sell junk to vendors. Quest rewards are your main gear upgrades — for melee and hunters, a weapon upgrade beats everything else.

    Staying aliveTap to open⌄

    Eat food and drink water between fights to refill health and mana — everyone does, it's not a weakness. Pulling one extra enemy is the #1 cause of death for new players. Pull small until a fight feels boring.

    Hearthstone, rested XP, and campfiresTap to open⌄

    Your Hearthstone is a stone in your bag that teleports you back to one chosen inn (10-second cast, then a 1-hour cooldown before you can use it again). Talk to the innkeeper in every new quest town and set your Hearthstone there — it's your 'go home' button at the end of a session.

    Logging out inside an inn or a capital city builds up rested XP — a bonus that makes kills worth DOUBLE experience until it runs out. It builds a little every few hours, up to about a level and a half. Logging out in an inn is free progress while you sleep.

    Campfires are new in WoW Forever: around level 5 a quest teaches you Basic Campfire, from the Cooking skill. Camps and campfires out in the world also give rested XP and useful buffs, and other players can add to your fire — worth learning early.

    Travel — and your first mount at 40Tap to open⌄

    At the start, you walk. Everywhere. That's normal — the world is built for it, and quests chain from town to town so you're rarely walking without a purpose.

    Flight paths are your fast travel: talk to the flight master in each new town to unlock that stop, and from then on you can pay a few coins to fly between any two stops you've unlocked. Unlock every flight master you pass — future you says thanks.

    Boats (Alliance) and zeppelins (Horde) connect the continents and are free — just stand on board and wait for the ride. Ask a city guard for directions to the docks or the zeppelin tower.

    At level 40 you can learn riding and buy your first mount — about 100 gold all-in — and travel speed jumps for good. It's the big purchase of your leveling life: start saving in your 30s. A much faster epic mount exists at level 60, at around 1,000 gold — that's a long-term goal, not something to stress about now.

    Money basicsTap to open⌄

    Loot everything and sell the grey junk to any vendor. It feels like pennies because it is — but pennies are exactly how the level-40 mount gets paid for.

    Don't buy gear from vendors. Vendor weapons and armor are overpriced and weak; quest rewards and enemy drops are your gear. The one early purchase that IS worth it: bigger bags. More bag space means fewer town trips and less loot left behind.

    Capital cities have an Auction House, where players buy and sell from each other. While leveling, your main use for it is selling — materials you gather (ore, herbs, leather, cloth) are worth real money to other players, often more than you'd expect.

    Repair your gear at vendors before it breaks, and keep a small gold cushion. Running completely dry is the only real money trap at low levels.

    Dying — and getting backTap to open⌄

    When you die, you become a ghost at the nearest graveyard, and you run back to your body to come back to life where you fell. The run is annoying on purpose — it's the game's main punishment for dying — but it costs you nothing but time.

    A Spirit Healer at the graveyard can revive you instantly instead, but it damages your gear and — above level 10 — leaves you weakened (resurrection sickness: your stats and damage drop hard) for up to 10 minutes. Running back is almost always the better deal.

    Your gear also takes a little damage every time you die, so deaths quietly cost repair money too. One more reason the guides keep saying it: pull one enemy at a time.

    Groups & dungeonsIn a dungeon group · Loot manners — Need, Greed, Pass — tap to open⌄
    In a dungeon groupTap to open⌄

    Five players: one tank (holds the enemies' attention), one healer, three damage. Your job as damage: let the tank pull first, then attack the same enemy the tank is fighting. Don't run ahead, and don't hit enemies nobody else is fighting.

    Loot manners — Need, Greed, PassTap to open⌄

    When something good drops in a group, a window pops up and everyone picks one: Need, Greed, or Pass. Players who pick Need roll first; if nobody needs it, the Greed players roll; Pass means 'not for me.'

    Need = it's an upgrade for the character you're playing right now, and you'll equip it. Greed = you'd sell it, use it on a different character, or just want the money. Pass = you don't want it at all.

    The one rule that keeps groups friendly: only Need on gear for the spec you're playing in that run. If you want something for a different build, say so in chat and ask — most groups say yes, and asking costs you nothing.

    Not sure? Greed is always safe. Nobody ever got kicked for greeding; people absolutely get remembered for needing everything.

    Rulesets & PvPRulesets & PvP — fighting other players — tap to open⌄
    Rulesets & PvP — fighting other playersTap to open⌄

    Forever's worlds come in rulesets instead of separate realms. On a <b>Normal</b> ruleset, other players can only fight you if you choose to join in. On a <b>PvP</b> ruleset, players from the enemy faction can attack you out in the world — and you can attack them. Your whole group must pick the SAME ruleset to play together (see 'Play together' above), so decide this before anyone creates a character.

    <b>Which should new players pick?</b> Normal, for a first group. Learning the game is challenge enough without enemy players camping your quest area. A PvP ruleset is for players who WANT that open-world danger as part of the game — some people love it, but go in knowing that's the deal.

    <b>How world PvP works.</b> In contested and enemy territory, an enemy-faction player can attack you at any time — and near their towns, their guards will join in. Your own starting zones are safe ground: enemies can't easily reach you there. If you do get jumped, treat it like a bad pull: run toward your own town or guards, use your escape buttons, or fight back.

    <b>Fights you choose.</b> On ANY ruleset you can duel a friend (a friendly one-on-one that stops before anyone dies), and battlegrounds offer organized team-vs-team PvP matches with objectives like capture-the-flag — player-vs-player on purpose, with even teams, instead of ambushes.

    🛠️ WoW Forever Professions

    Jobs your character learns alongside fighting: gathering raw materials, crafting gear and potions, and three side skills everyone gets. You pick TWO primary professions — this section helps you pick well. Checked against WoW Forever launch info — Nov 15, 2026.

    Start hereHow professions work — read this first · The beginner default — if you don't want to think about it — tap to open⌄
    How professions work — read this firstTap to open⌄

    The two-slot rule. Every character can learn TWO primary professions (any mix of gathering and crafting) AND all three secondary professions (Cooking, First Aid, Fishing). Secondary ones never use a primary slot — learn all three, always.

    Skill and ranks. Each profession runs from skill 1 to 300. Trainers promote you through four ranks as you grow: Apprentice (to 75), Journeyman (to 150), Expert (to 225), Artisan (to 300). Recipes show in colors: orange recipes almost always raise your skill, yellow usually, green sometimes, grey never.

    Learning and unlearning. You learn professions — and most recipes — from trainers in towns and capital cities. You can drop a profession at any time to make room for another, but its skill resets to zero: if you ever relearn it, you start over. Choose like you mean it.

    A head start. Each starting zone hides an early quest item that teaches you one gathering profession (Herbalism, Mining, or Skinning) — watch your bags in your first levels.

    New in Forever. Professions got bigger here: 600+ new recipes, advanced Blueprint recipes that drop from dungeon bosses, crafted food with an XP bonus, and the Camping system — from skill 20, most professions can place a Camp Feature at a campfire that buffs everyone sitting there. Crafting professions also earn account-wide Legacy Points at skill 150, 225, and 300 — one more reason gathering-only characters eventually want a craft.

    The beginner default — if you don't want to think about itTap to open⌄

    The answer. Take Skinning + Herbalism (or Mining) while you level. Sell everything you gather on the Auction House. Decide on crafting later, when you're rich or level 60.

    Why it works. Gathering costs nothing — no recipes, no materials, no trainer bills — and launch economies pay top prices for raw materials because every crafter needs them. Your two gathering professions quietly pay for your level-40 mount, which is the purchase that actually matters.

    When to switch. At 60 (or once the mount is bought), drop one gathering profession for the craft that fits your class — Alchemy for potions, your armor craft to gear yourself — and keep the gathering profession that feeds it. Dropping is free; only the dropped skill is lost.

    The one exception. Cloth casters (Mage, Priest, Warlock): Tailoring is so cheap to level — your materials drop from normal enemies — that Tailoring + one gathering profession is an equally good default for you.

    Gathering — free materials, sell or feed a craftHerbalism · Mining · Skinning — tap to open⌄
    HerbalismTap to open⌄

    What it is. You pick herbs growing in the wild — flowers and plants shown on your mini-map once you turn on Find Herbs. Herbs are the main ingredient for Alchemy, and some feed First Aid's potions.

    What you actually get. A steady stream of sellable herbs, plus the herbs to power an Alchemist friend (or yourself). At a campfire, Herbalism's Camp Feature is the Incense Candle, which raises Intellect for campers.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing — no tools, no recipes to buy. You gather while you quest, so it levels itself. One of the two easiest professions in the game.

    Pairs with. Alchemy (it eats your herbs), or Skinning/Mining for a double-gathering money setup.

    Take it if. You want income with zero effort, or anyone in your group is an Alchemist.

    Beginner verdict. Top-tier first pick. Take it and never regret it.

    MiningTap to open⌄

    What it is. You dig ore, stone, and the occasional gem out of rock veins, using a Mining Pick. You can also smelt ore into metal bars, which is how Blacksmithing and Engineering actually consume it.

    What you actually get. Ore and bars that always sell — smiths and engineers need a constant supply — plus stone used for sharpening stones and explosives.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing but the pick. Veins cluster around hills, caves, and zone edges, so it takes slightly more detouring than Herbalism.

    Pairs with. Blacksmithing or Engineering (both eat bars), or Herbalism for the classic double-gathering money pair.

    Take it if. You want the most reliable Auction House income, or you're feeding a smith/engineer.

    Beginner verdict. Top-tier first pick, tied with Herbalism. Mining + Herbalism is the launch money standard.

    SkinningTap to open⌄

    What it is. After a beast dies, you skin its corpse with a Skinning Knife and take its leather and hide. Leather feeds Leatherworking; everything also sells.

    What you actually get. Leather from the animals you're already killing for quests — income that happens by itself, with no nodes to hunt down.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing but the knife. The only catch: it only works on beasts, so it shines in beast-heavy zones and does nothing in, say, an undead crypt.

    Pairs with. Leatherworking (it eats your leather), or Herbalism/Mining for double gathering.

    Take it if. Your class wears leather (Rogue, Druid) or you just want the laziest money in the game.

    Beginner verdict. The easiest money profession while leveling. Skin everything you kill.

    Crafting — turn materials into gear and powerAlchemy · Blacksmithing · Enchanting · Engineering · Leatherworking · Tailoring — tap to open⌄
    AlchemyTap to open⌄

    What it is. You brew herbs into potions (instant effects — healing, mana, invisibility), elixirs (stat boosts that last through a fight), and at high skill, flasks (powerful boosts that persist through death) and transmutations (turning one material into another, on a cooldown). Note: in Forever, the standard Healing Potions moved to First Aid — Alchemy's strength is buffs, mana, and utility.

    What you actually get. The consumables every dungeon and raid group burns through constantly — which means permanent demand for what you make. At a campfire, Alchemy's Camp Feature is the Mana Well, restoring mana to campers.

    Cost & effort. Moderate: recipes from trainers plus some rare ones from vendors and drops. Needs a constant herb supply — bring Herbalism or a Herbalist friend.

    Pairs with. Herbalism. This is the most natural pair in the game.

    Take it if. You want to be the group's potion supply, or you like steady Auction House income from things people consume and rebuy.

    Beginner verdict. The most group-friendly crafting profession, and the safest crafting pick for a new player.

    BlacksmithingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You forge metal bars into mail and plate armor and most melee weapons, plus sharpening stones and weightstones that temporarily boost a weapon. You'll need an anvil (found in every town) for most recipes.

    What you actually get. Gear for warriors and paladins — the two classes that can't easily gear themselves any other way — including some of the best pre-raid weapons and armor at 60.

    Cost & effort. Expensive in materials: it eats ore and bars constantly. Without Mining (or a rich alt), you'll buy your materials and feel it.

    Pairs with. Mining. Non-negotiable for a first character.

    Take it if. You're a Warrior or Paladin who wants to wear your own work, or your group's plate wearers need a smith.

    Beginner verdict. Great on a Warrior/Paladin with Mining. Painful without the gathering half.

    EnchantingTap to open⌄

    What it is. Two jobs in one: you DISENCHANT magic items (green quality or better) and break them down into dusts, essences, and shards — then you spend those materials putting permanent stat boosts (enchants) on weapons and armor: yours, your group's, anyone's.

    What you actually get. The only way to upgrade gear you already own, and a service other players pay well for at 60. Every enchant you do consumes materials you got by destroying items.

    Cost & effort. The most expensive profession to level: every skill point comes from items you could have sold. New players feel this one in the wallet.

    Pairs with. Tailoring — the classic self-sufficient duo: tailors craft green items from dropped cloth, and you disenchant them for materials. No gathering needed.

    Take it if. You like being useful to everyone at 60 and don't mind a slower, pricier road getting there.

    Beginner verdict. Powerful but pricey. On a first character, consider taking it as your SECOND character's job — or pair it with Tailoring and be patient.

    EngineeringTap to open⌄

    What it is. You build gadgets: bombs and grenades, target dummies, scopes for guns and bows, jumper cables that can restart a dead player's heart (sometimes), and goggles only engineers can wear. Many items only work for engineers.

    What you actually get. Fun and power that no other profession gives — extra stuns, burst damage, and utility that shines in PvP and saves dungeon runs. Also the game's best toys.

    Cost & effort. Expensive and gloriously impractical: it eats ore, stone, and odd parts, and most of what you make can't be sold to non-engineers. You do this one for yourself.

    Pairs with. Mining — it feeds on bars and stone.

    Take it if. You want your character to feel different from everyone else's, and you accept that it's a gold sink, not a gold source.

    Beginner verdict. The fun pick, not the rich pick. Wonderful second profession once your mount is paid for.

    LeatherworkingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You turn leather and hides into leather armor (Rogues, Druids) and mail armor (Hunters, Shamans), plus armor kits that add a small permanent armor boost to a piece of gear.

    What you actually get. A full self-gearing path for four classes, and armor kits everyone can use. Forever massively expanded its recipe list — it's a deeper profession here than it was in old Classic.

    Cost & effort. Moderate: leather is cheap if you skin it yourself, pricey if you don't. Some recipes also want thread and dyes from vendors.

    Pairs with. Skinning. Leather is the whole diet.

    Take it if. You play Rogue, Druid, Hunter, or Shaman and want to wear what you make.

    Beginner verdict. Solid with Skinning on the right class. Skip it on cloth and plate classes.

    TailoringTap to open⌄

    What it is. You sew cloth — which drops from humanoid enemies everywhere — into cloth armor for Mages, Priests, and Warlocks, shirts, and BAGS. Bags are the headline: bigger bags are every player's first real purchase, and tailors make them.

    What you actually get. Gear for your cloth caster with NO gathering profession needed (the world drops your materials), plus a product — bags — that sells at every level, forever.

    Cost & effort. The cheapest crafting profession to level, because cloth falls out of normal questing. Some high-end patterns drop from enemies or come from vendors.

    Pairs with. Enchanting (make greens, disenchant them) — or any gathering profession, since Tailoring doesn't need the slot.

    Take it if. You're a Mage, Priest, or Warlock — or you just want the bag business.

    Beginner verdict. The friendliest crafting profession for a first character. Cloth class? Take it.

    CookingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You cook raw meat and fish into food that restores health faster — and good food adds a 'Well Fed' stat buff for 15+ minutes. In Forever, crafted food also grants a small XP bonus, so eating before a quest run literally levels you faster.

    What you actually get. Cheaper, stronger recovery than vendor food, real stat buffs, and the campfire itself: Cooking's Basic Campfire is what creates a campsite where everyone's Camp Features work.

    Cost & effort. Nearly free: your materials are the meat from beasts you already kill. Learn it at level 5-ish and keep it current — it's the first profession a new Forever player touches.

    Pairs with. Fishing (fish are prime cooking ingredients) and every beast you ever kill.

    Take it if. Everyone. It's secondary — it doesn't use one of your two primary slots. There is no reason not to.

    Beginner verdict. Mandatory. Learn it on day one.

    First AidTap to open⌄

    What it is. You turn cloth into bandages that heal you mid-adventure without mana or a healer — bandage between fights, or even during a fight if nothing is hitting you. Forever expanded it: First Aid now also crafts the standard Healing Potions and cures for poison and disease.

    What you actually get. Self-healing for the classes that have none (Warrior, Rogue, Hunter, Mage) and an emergency heal for everyone else. In dungeons, a bandage can save a run when the healer is out of mana.

    Cost & effort. Nearly free: cloth is everywhere. It competes with Tailoring for cloth — if you're both, bandages for leveling, sewing for selling.

    Pairs with. Everything. It's secondary — no primary slot used.

    Take it if. Everyone, but it's life-changing for Warriors and Rogues especially.

    Beginner verdict. Mandatory. The closest thing professions have to a cheat code for staying alive.

    FishingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You cast a line into any water with a Fishing Pole and catch fish — ingredients for Cooking and Alchemy — plus the occasional locked box, useful item, or junk you'll laugh at.

    What you actually get. Cooking ingredients you'd otherwise buy, some fish that Alchemists pay well for, and the most relaxing money in the game. At a campfire, Fishing's Camp Feature is the Fishing Bowl.

    Cost & effort. Free, slow, and proud of it. Skill rises by catching, and higher-level waters need higher skill (and sometimes a lure).

    Pairs with. Cooking — catch it, cook it, eat the buff.

    Take it if. You like a calm grind, or your Cooking keeps running out of fish.

    Beginner verdict. Optional but charming. Learn it when you want a break from killing things.

    How dungeons work here — read this firstTap to open⌄

    Five players, three jobs. A dungeon group is a party of five: one tank, one healer, three damage dealers. If those words are new, the General Info tab explains them (Groups & dungeons, and the Combat foundations cards — interrupts and aggro are the two skills dungeons test most).

    No dungeon finder. There is no automatic queue in Forever. You form a group yourselves (friends, guild, or the LookingForGroup chat channel), then you all TRAVEL to the dungeon's entrance and walk in. The trip is part of the game — go together, especially through enemy territory.

    Quests are the real reward. Forever deliberately moved dungeon experience INTO the quests: monster XP is the smaller share (a launch hotfix raised it somewhat), quest XP is the big payout. The golden rule: collect ALL of a dungeon's quests, run it once, hand them all in. Running a dungeon with no quests is doing it the slow way.

    Boss loot got better. In Forever, every dungeon boss drops rare (blue) quality gear, and much of it belongs to sets (like the Defias Leather set in the Deadmines) that give bonuses when you wear several pieces. Dungeon gear is a real gearing path now, not a side trip.

    Stay in the level range. A hotfix rule: if the enemies are grey (far below) ANY group member, the whole party earns no experience from them. Group with people near your level and run dungeons in their range.

    Blueprints. Dungeon bosses can drop Blueprint recipes for crafting professions — one more reason your group's crafter wants to come along (see the Professions tab).

    Ragefire ChasmTap to open⌄

    Where. Inside Orgrimmar itself — the entrance is in the Cleft of Shadow, in the Horde capital. No travel, no danger getting there (for Horde).

    Levels & size. 13–18, five players, about 30 minutes. The Horde's first dungeon and the gentlest introduction to group play in the game.

    Before you go. Pick up the quests inside Orgrimmar (they're all nearby). Bring food and water; there's nothing else to prepare — this one is the tutorial.

    Oggleflint. A big trogg. Tank him facing away from the group; nothing fancy.

    Taragaman the Hungerer. A fire demon. Spread out a little so his fire nova doesn't catch everyone, and burn him down.

    Jergosh the Invoker & Bazzalan. Warlock-type bosses with helper imps and cultists — kill the adds first, interrupt their casts when you can.

    Watch out. Lava crawlers patrol; pull them alone. Otherwise this dungeon is forgiving by design.

    Levels & size. 13–18, five players. One of the two first dungeons in the game, and the Alliance's starter.

    Before you go. Grab the dungeon quests in Ironforge and Dun Morogh first — in Forever, the quests are where most of a run's experience lives.

    Watch out. Horde visitors: the trip in is the dangerous part on a PvP ruleset, not the dungeon.

    Wailing CavernsTap to open⌄

    Where. In the Barrens, in an oasis cave south-west of the Crossroads. The entrance cave itself has enemies — fight your way to the glowing portal.

    Levels & size. 15–24, five players, and LONG — budget 1 to 1.5 hours. A maze of caverns full of druids gone wrong.

    Before you go. It has around 7 quests (mostly Horde, from the Crossroads, Ratchet, and Thunder Bluff; Alliance get fewer). Stack them all — one full clear with every quest is a level or more of experience. Bring water; casters drink a lot here.

    The four Lords of the Fang. Lady Anacondra, Lord Cobrahn, Lord Pythas, and Lord Serpentis — corrupted druid bosses spread through the maze. All four must die to unlock the ending. Interrupt their lightning and healing when you can.

    Kresh & Skum. A giant turtle and a thunder lizard — simple tank-and-kill fights on the way.

    Verdan the Everliving. A huge elemental who hits the tank VERY hard for this level. The healer's whole job is this fight; everyone else just burns him.

    Mutanus the Devourer. The final boss, and he doesn't exist until you finish: after the four Lords die, talk to the Disciple of Naralex at the entrance and escort him to the back — survive the waves, then Mutanus rises.

    Watch out. It's easy to get lost — stay together and mark your tank. Deviate beasts sleep and fear; pull carefully. The Embrace of the Viper gear set drops here and is worth wearing.

    The DeadminesTap to open⌄

    Where. In Westfall — enter through the barn in Moonbrook and fight through the mine tunnels to the portal. The Alliance's signature first dungeon.

    Levels & size. 17–26, five players, about an hour. A pirate-brotherhood stronghold that ends on a ship.

    Before you go. Do the Westfall Defias quest chain first — it unlocks the dungeon's main quests (about 7 in total, worth a huge chunk of XP in Forever). Kill miners on the way in; they drop a powder used for a door later.

    Rhahk'Zor. An ogre foreman with two helpers — kill the helpers, then him.

    Sneed. A goblin in a giant shredder machine. Destroy the shredder, then Sneed himself hops out — finish him.

    Gilnid & Captain Greenskin. The smelter boss and the ship's first mate — straightforward fights; Greenskin poisons, so the healer watches the tank.

    Mr. Smite. The ship's tauren guard. Twice during the fight he STUNS the whole group and runs to his chest for a bigger weapon. You can't stop it — just survive the stun and keep going. He hits much harder each time.

    Edwin VanCleef. The final boss on the ship's top deck, leader of the Defias. Near the end he calls two bodyguards — kill them, then finish him. The Defias Leather set drops in here.

    Watch out. Patrols walk the tunnels BEHIND you after you pass. Clear as you go and don't run back alone.

    Shadowfang KeepTap to open⌄

    Where. In Silverpine Forest, on a cliff west of the Sepulcher — a haunted castle full of worgen (werewolves).

    Levels & size. 22–30, five players, about 45 minutes. Spooky, compact, and a favorite.

    Before you go. Horde get the main quests from the Undercity/Sepulcher; Alliance have a couple too. Bring someone who can cure curses if you have one — the ghosts love cursing.

    Commander Springvale. A ghost knight with two guards. Kill the guards first; his holy spells hurt.

    Odo the Blindwatcher & Fenrus. A bat-handler and a huge wolf. Fenrus hits hard and fast — the tank's fight, everyone else burns.

    Archmage Arugal. The final boss and the man who cursed this place. He teleports around his tower, blasts the group with shadow, and can briefly turn one of you into a worgen. Keep pressure on him wherever he appears and heal through it.

    Blackfathom DeepsTap to open⌄

    Where. On the coast of Ashenvale — you swim down through a flooded temple entrance to reach the portal. Yes, you swim; don't panic.

    Levels & size. 24–32, five players, about an hour. An underwater temple of naga and Twilight cultists.

    Before you go. Quests from Ashenvale and (for Horde) the Stonetalon foothills. Light the braziers as you go — the dungeon's torch puzzle opens the way forward.

    Ghamoo-ra. A giant turtle in a pool. Simple, but his shell makes him slow to kill — patience.

    Twilight Lord Kelris. The dangerous one: he puts the tank (or whoever he chooses) to SLEEP mid-fight. Whoever isn't asleep keeps fighting; the sleeper wakes after a few seconds. Kill his cultist adds first.

    Aku'mai. A three-headed hydra in the final pool — the tank holds it at the pool's edge and everyone burns it down.

    Watch out. Murlocs. They flee at low health and bring friends. Kill runners fast, or crowd-control them.

    Where. Inside Stormwind itself — a prison in the Canals district. Alliance home dungeon; Horde would have to invade the capital, so it's Alliance-only in practice.

    Levels & size. 24–32, five players, and SHORT — about 30 minutes. Three wings of rioting prisoners.

    Before you go. The quests come from Stormwind and the Redridge/Westfall chains (the riot's backstory). This dungeon's loot is thin — you run it for the quest XP, which in Forever is the point.

    Kam Deepfury, Targorr the Dread, Dextren Ward. The three wing bosses — all simple tank-and-kill fights. Hamhock, an ogre at the back of one wing, is the only one who hits notably hard.

    Scarlet MonasteryTap to open⌄

    Where. In Tirisfal Glades, north-east of the Undercity — a monastery of fanatical Scarlet Crusade humans. It has FOUR separate wings, each its own dungeon.

    Levels & size. 26–45 across the wings: Graveyard 26–36, Library 29–39, Armory 32–42, Cathedral 35–45. Five players, 30–45 minutes per wing. You'll return here across many levels.

    Before you go. Horde get the key quests from the Undercity; the Library drops the Scarlet Key that opens the Armory and Cathedral doors. Do the wings in order: Graveyard → Library → Armory → Cathedral.

    Bloodmage Thalnos (Graveyard). An undead caster who raises skeletons and rains fire. Kill the raised adds, interrupt his fire when you can.

    Arcanist Doan (Library). A mage who, at half health, casts a huge Arcane Explosion — when he starts casting it, EVERYONE runs away from him, then runs back in.

    Herod (Armory). The famous one. He periodically spins in a whirlwind ('Blades of Light') — melee step OUT while he spins, step back in when he stops. At the end he enrages; burn him.

    Mograine & Whitemane (Cathedral). Kill Commander Mograine first. When he falls, High Inquisitor Whitemane sleeps your whole group and RESURRECTS him — wake up, kill Mograine again, then kill her. She also heals, so interrupt her when possible.

    Watch out. Scarlet runners flee and bring whole rooms. In the Cathedral, clear the entire hall before pulling the bosses, or the whole room joins the fight.

    UldamanTap to open⌄

    Where. In the Badlands, in an ancient titan vault the dwarves are excavating. There's a back entrance in the north of the zone once you know it.

    Levels & size. 41–51, five players, about 1–1.5 hours. The last bosses are much higher level than the first — groups often do it in two visits.

    Before you go. A long quest web (Badlands, Ironforge/Orgrimmar, and a chain that starts inside). Two items combine into the staff that opens the final chamber — the group needs the pieces from mid-bosses, so don't leave early.

    Zul'FarrakTap to open⌄

    Where. In Tanaris, in the desert — a sand troll city, mostly open-air. Bring sunscreen jokes; everyone makes them.

    Levels & size. 44–54, five players, about an hour. A beloved dungeon with one of the game's most famous fights.

    Before you go. Quests from Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port. One boss (Gahz'rilla) needs a special mallet from a quest chain that starts in the Hinterlands — if your group has it, great; if not, skip that boss.

    Where. In the Swamp of Sorrows, in a lake — the Temple of Atal'Hakkar, a troll temple sunk into the water. The real dungeon is the upper levels inside.

    Levels & size. 50–60, five players, 1–1.5 hours. A puzzle-temple: the layout confuses every first-timer, guaranteed.

    Blackrock DepthsTap to open⌄

    Where. Inside Blackrock Mountain (between the Burning Steppes and Searing Gorge) — a whole Dark Iron dwarf CITY underground. The biggest dungeon in the game.

    Levels & size. 52–60, five players, and enormous: a full clear takes 3+ hours. Nobody does it in one sitting — it's really two dungeons: the Detention Block (lower) and the Upper City (emperor).

    Before you go. Get the Shadowforge Key: its quest chain starts from a ghost you can only talk to while DEAD near the mountain's central chamber (die once on purpose nearby — really). The key opens shortcuts that make repeat visits sane. There are 15+ quests; stack them across multiple visits.

    Lord Incendius. A fire elemental on a bridge of lava — the tank keeps him out of the lava; everyone else stays out of his fire nova range or heals through it.

    The Lyceum. A room of endless fast-respawning dwarves where you must light two braziers with torches dropped by special enemies. Constant motion, no stopping — the game's first 'keep moving' room.

    Emperor Dagran Thaurissan. The final boss, in the throne room, with Princess Moira beside him. If you have the quest to save her, DON'T kill her — keep her alive (she heals him, annoyingly) and burn the Emperor; if not, kill her first. Know which run you're doing BEFORE the pull.

    Watch out. The Grim Guzzler bar halfway through can be crossed peacefully (buy the barman a drink / do a small quest) or as a giant bar fight. Choose deliberately — the fight is fun, the shortcut is faster.

    Where. In Feralas — a ruined night elf city with THREE separate wings: East, West, and North, each its own dungeon with its own entrance.

    Levels & size. 55–60, five players, about an hour per wing. Released as the 'catch-up' dungeon set: its gear is excellent for fresh 60s.

    Before you go. East is the easiest start. West needs the Crescent Key (from East's imp-chase boss, Pusillin — chase him through the dungeon as he runs). North has the famous alternative: the TRIBUTE run.

    Alzzin the Wildshaper (East). A satyr who shapeshifts and summons plant adds at the end of a garden courtyard. Kill the adds as they sprout.

    Prince Tortheldrin (West). The final boss of West, guarded by a force field: you must deactivate the pylons around the library (each guarded) to drop his shield. His demon advisor Immol'thar comes first — an eye-beast that beams the group.

    King Gordok (North) & the tribute run. If you kill every North boss, you fight the King with his shaman Cho'Rush helping. But if you SKIP the other bosses (with tricks: an ogre suit costume, freezing one boss in a trap), the King dies alone and the surviving bosses PAY YOU TRIBUTE — a chest of extra loot. The tribute run is the connoisseur's clear.

    Watch out. Ogre packs in North hit like trucks and the patrols are dense. West is caster-heavy — interrupts matter again.

    Where. In the Western Plaguelands, on the island of Caer Darrow — a school of necromancy in a haunted estate. The door needs the Skeleton Key (a quest chain through both Plaguelands).
