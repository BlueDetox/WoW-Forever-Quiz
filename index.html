    WarriorArms · Fury — 2 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Warrior — ArmsMeleeFirst-timer: MediumOne big two-handed weapon. Slow heavy hits + wounds that bleed over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Rend. Open with this — a wound that keeps bleeding over time.
    2. Heroic Strike. Your filler hit when you have spare rage (rage is the energy you build up by hitting enemies and being hit).
    3. Overpower. Use it whenever it lights up — it triggers when an enemy dodges you.
    4. Victory Rush. From level 20: use it right after a kill — it gives you health back.
    5. Mortal Strike. At level 40 this becomes your biggest hit. It also cuts the enemy's healing in half for a short time.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Battle Stance is your home while leveling — stay in it.
    • Sweeping Strikes (level 30) makes your hits splash onto a second nearby enemy, so two-enemy fights get much easier.

    Leveling tips

    • Your weapon IS your character: a better two-handed weapon is a bigger upgrade than any armor piece. Take weapon quest rewards first.
    • On gear, look for Strength first, then Stamina. Eat food between fights — you have almost no self-healing.

    Watch out

    • Pulling two or three enemies at once is how new warriors die. Pull one, finish it, eat, repeat.
    • Don't open every fight at full sprint — walking in with no rage means a slow start. It builds as the fight goes.
    Warrior — FuryMeleeFirst-timer: MediumTwo weapons, fast non-stop hits. Spin attack hits enemies around you.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Battle Shout. Keep this buff on yourself — it raises your attack power.
    2. Bloodthirst. Your main hit. Use it every time it's ready.
    3. Whirlwind. Spin and hit every enemy around you — great when two enemies are on you.
    4. Heroic Strike. Burns your extra rage on a harder hit.
    5. Execute. Only works on enemies under 20% health — it's your finisher. Save it for the end of a fight.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Two weapons means more hits, which means more rage — Fury fights speed up as they go.
    • Expect to miss more than an Arms warrior early on. Two-weapon fighting starts inaccurate and gets better with gear.

    Leveling tips

    • Keep BOTH weapons upgraded, not just one — a weak off-hand weapon drags the whole build down.
    • On gear: Strength and Agility. Agility also gives you critical hits, which Fury loves.

    Watch out

    • You still have no real self-healing. If a fight goes bad, run early — don't wait until you're almost dead.
    • Whirlwind feels great and pulls extra enemies by accident. Check what's standing near your target before you spin.
    PaladinRetribution — 1 damage option, tap to open⌄
    Paladin — RetributionMeleeFirst-timer: EasyHoly knight, big weapon. Can heal itself + a bubble makes it immune briefly.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Keep a Seal active. A Seal adds holy damage to every swing. Once you learn Seal of Command, that's your main one.
    2. Holy Strike. From level 6 — a holy melee hit. One of your first real buttons.
    3. Judgement. Spends your Seal's power on a burst of holy damage. In WoW Forever your Seal stays up afterwards, so judge freely.
    4. Consecration. From level 20 — lays holy fire on the ground. Use it when enemies stand grouped together.
    5. Hammer of Wrath. A ranged holy hammer that only works on enemies under 20% health — your finisher.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Seal of Fury (level 10) adds holy damage to your swings and gives you a small damage shield on top.
    • Your bubble (Divine Shield) makes you immune for a few seconds. It's your panic button — hit it, heal, then keep fighting.
    • You can heal yourself with your holy spells between fights. That's your big solo advantage over a warrior.

    Leveling tips

    • Use a slow, heavy two-handed weapon — Seal of Command hits much harder with slow weapons.
    • On gear: Strength and Stamina first, with some Intellect so you have mana to heal.

    Watch out

    • You have no interrupt and no slow: enemy casters will finish their spells, and runners will run. Finish runners with Hammer of Wrath or Judgement.
    • Don't spend all your mana healing in the middle of a fight — an empty paladin can't fight back. Heal between fights instead.
    HunterBeast Mastery · Marksmanship · Survival — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Hunter — Beast MasteryRanged + PetFirst-timer: EasyYour pet takes the hits for you while you shoot from far away. Easiest alone.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Hunter's Mark. Put this on the enemy first — it takes extra damage from you.
    2. Send your pet in. Your pet attacks first and uses Growl, which forces the enemy to fight the pet instead of you.
    3. Serpent Sting. A poison shot that keeps hurting over time. Put it on and leave it.
    4. Arcane Shot. Instant damage, no waiting. Your main shot while leveling.
    5. Bestial Wrath. A talent later in the tree: your pet rages for 18 seconds, dealing 50% more damage and impossible to stop. Save it for hard fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • You tame your first real pet at level 10 — and in WoW Forever you can tame beasts up to 2 levels higher than you. Visit a pet trainer to teach it Growl and other skills.
    • Your pet gets stronger from YOUR gear, so your upgrades are its upgrades too.
    • At level 20 all hunters learn Aimed Shot. It shares a cooldown with Multi-Shot, so pick one per situation.
    • If you take the Summon Hawk talent, it shares a cooldown with Arcane Shot — one or the other each time.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility above everything — it powers you and your pet.
    • Traps work during combat in WoW Forever. A Freezing Trap can park one extra enemy while your pet handles another.

    Watch out

    • If your pet dies, everything runs straight at a lightly-armored you. Heal it with Mend Pet during hard fights, and let it get the enemy's attention before you start shooting.
    Hunter — MarksmanshipRangedFirst-timer: EasyA sniper. Slow, big shots from far away. Can play with no pet for +20% damage.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Hunter's Mark. Always first — marked enemies take more damage from you.
    2. Aimed Shot. From level 20: your huge shot. It takes 2 seconds to fire and you must stand still — open fights with it.
    3. Arcane Shot. Instant filler damage between bigger shots.
    4. Multi-Shot. Hits up to 3 nearby enemies. It shares a cooldown with Aimed Shot: Multi-Shot for groups, Aimed Shot for one target.
    5. Auto Shot. Your bow or gun keeps firing on its own — as long as you keep your distance and give it a moment standing still to fire.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • The Lone Wolf talent gives you +20% damage with NO pet at all. It's strong, but nothing holds enemies off you — only take it once keeping your distance feels easy.
    • Sniper Shot, at 31 points in the tree, is this spec's long-range finisher talent.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first. A slower bow or gun with bigger damage numbers usually beats a fast, weak one.
    • Fight at maximum range. Distance is your armor.

    Watch out

    • In WoW Forever your Auto Shot needs a brief stand-still before it fires — constant stutter-stepping quietly kills your damage. Plant your feet, shoot, then move.
    • If an enemy reaches you, your shots get interrupted. Slow it (Concussive Shot), back up, and reset your range.
    Hunter — SurvivalMelee + PetFirst-timer: MediumFights up close next to its pet. Sets traps that hold or hurt enemies.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Open from range. Start with your shots the way every hunter does — then stay in close and keep fighting.
    2. Raptor Strike. A hard melee hit. One of your main close-range buttons.
    3. Mongoose Bite. A counter-hit that becomes available in melee. With the Lacerating Strikes talent, it also makes the enemy bleed.
    4. Wing Clip. Slows an enemy's movement — use it on anything trying to run, or to buy yourself space.
    5. Your traps. Freezing Trap parks an enemy in ice; Immolation Trap sets one on fire. In WoW Forever traps work in combat, and they're real weapons for you.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Expose Prey (talent) can set up Mongoose Bite without waiting for a dodge — the tree is built around that combo.
    • You're the only hunter who wants to be in melee. Your pet still matters: it holds one enemy while you duel another.

    Leveling tips

    • You need TWO good weapons: your bow/gun and your melee weapon. Don't let either one fall behind.
    • On gear: Agility and Stamina — you take more hits than other hunters.

    Watch out

    • You wear hunter armor, not warrior armor. Dueling two enemies at once in melee is a loss — trap one first, then fight the other.
    RogueAssassination · Combat · Subtlety — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Rogue — AssassinationMeleeFirst-timer: MediumStabs with daggers. Poisons keep damaging the enemy over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Stealth opener. Start fights hidden. Ambush for a huge first hit, or Garrote to start a bleed.
    2. Sinister Strike. Your builder: each hit adds a combo point on the enemy (up to 5).
    3. Keep poisons on. Coat your weapons with poison and re-apply it when it wears off — a steady share of your damage comes from poison.
    4. Slice and Dice. Spend a couple of combo points on this early — you attack faster while it's running.
    5. Eviscerate. Spend 4–5 combo points on this finisher for your biggest hit.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Combo points are the rogue engine: builders add points, finishers spend them, and more points means a stronger finisher.
    • Your energy bar refills on its own over a few seconds. Starting a fight at full energy matters more than any single button.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first, then Strength. Keep your main-hand weapon sharp and current — most of your damage starts there.
    • Sap one enemy before fighting a group — it sleeps through the whole fight if nobody hits it.

    Watch out

    • You have no self-healing at all. Eat between fights, and if a pull goes wrong, Vanish and walk away instead of dying bravely.
    Rogue — CombatMeleeFirst-timer: MediumMore head-on than other rogues, less sneaking. Steady sword / axe attacks.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Sinister Strike. Your builder — adds combo points. This is most of your button presses.
    2. Slice and Dice. Spend your first few combo points here: attacking faster means more of everything.
    3. Eviscerate. Your finisher at 4–5 combo points.
    4. Blade Flurry. For two-enemy fights: your attacks also hit a second nearby enemy.
    5. Adrenaline Rush. The tree's big talent: your energy floods back for a short burst window. Save it for hard fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever, poisons and sharpening stones work TOGETHER instead of replacing each other — use both.
    • One-handed axes are rogue weapons here too, so swords, maces and axes are all fair game.
    • You stay in Stealth after using Sap in WoW Forever — sap, then pick your real target.

    Leveling tips

    • A slow main-hand weapon makes each Sinister Strike hit harder. On gear: Agility and Strength.
    • This is the head-on rogue — of the three, it spends the least time worrying about stealth.

    Watch out

    • Melee only and no self-heals, so eat between pulls. Two enemies at once without Blade Flurry ready is a bad trade.
    Rogue — SubtletyMeleeFirst-timer: HarderSneaks unseen and strikes from hiding. Wounds keep bleeding over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Stealth opener. Cheap Shot stuns the enemy as you open; Ambush is the huge-damage option with a dagger. Pick per fight.
    2. Hemorrhage. Your main attack out in the open — it wounds the enemy so it keeps suffering from your hits.
    3. Backstab. Only works from behind the enemy — and it's one of your hardest hits. Get behind whenever you can.
    4. Kidney Shot. Spends combo points to stun. Use it to stop a caster or to breathe mid-fight.
    5. Eviscerate. Your damage finisher at 4–5 combo points.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Preparation (talent) instantly refreshes your other cooldowns — that's a second Vanish and a second Blind in one fight. It's your reset button.
    • Elusiveness (talent) cuts the cooldowns of Vanish and Blind, so your escapes come back faster than other rogues'.

    Leveling tips

    • Positioning is damage: behind the enemy is where this spec lives. Circle as you fight.
    • Daggers make your stealth openers and Backstab hit their hardest. On gear: Agility first.

    Watch out

    • Your whole plan starts in stealth. If an enemy sees you first, the opener is gone and the fight is twice as hard — approach pulls slowly and from the side.
    PriestShadow — 1 damage option, tap to open⌄
    Priest — ShadowRangedFirst-timer: MediumDark spells hurt over time. Shadow form boosts them, damage heals group a bit.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Shadow Word: Pain. Always first — shadow damage that keeps ticking over time.
    2. Mind Blast. Your direct shadow hit. Use it whenever it's ready.
    3. Mind Flay. A channeled shadow beam that also slows the enemy's movement — good while it walks toward you.
    4. Shoot (your wand). Between spells, your wand does real damage. In WoW Forever you need to stand still for wand shots to fire.
    5. Shadow Word: Death. From level 32: a heavy finisher — use it to end fights.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Shadowform, the tree's form, makes your shadow spells cheaper in WoW Forever, so long fights strain your mana less.
    • Your damage trickle-heals your group a little while you fight (that's from your shadow talents) — in a group, you quietly patch everyone.
    • At level 20 every priest gets Devouring Plague (another damage-over-time spell) and Fear Ward (protection against being feared).
    • The Spirit Tap talent gives you mana back after kills — it's what keeps shadow leveling moving.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. And treat your wand like a real weapon — upgrade it whenever you can.
    • Shield yourself with Power Word: Shield BEFORE the pull starts, not in a panic during it.

    Watch out

    • You wear cloth — the lightest armor in the game. If two enemies reach you, you're in trouble: fear one away (Psychic Scream) and finish the other.
    ShamanElemental · Enhancement — 2 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Shaman — ElementalRangedFirst-timer: EasyLightning from far away + fire that keeps burning. Totems make your group stronger.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Flame Shock. Sets the enemy burning over time. Open with it.
    2. Lightning Bolt. Your main cast — most of your damage comes from this.
    3. Chain Lightning. When 2 or more enemies stand together, this jumps between them.
    4. Earth Shock. Instant damage — and it interrupts an enemy's spell cast. Save it for when you see an enemy casting something nasty.
    5. Lightning Shield. Keep this buff on yourself: attackers get zapped when they hit you.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • There's no Lava Burst in WoW Forever — this tree's big talent is Call of Thunder, which supercharges your lightning instead.
    • Totems are placeable helpers that last a short while. Searing Totem adds fire damage; others strengthen your whole group. Drop them when a fight will last, not for a 5-second kill.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Drink between pulls to refill mana.
    • You wear mail armor — tougher than cloth casters — but you're still a caster. Keep your distance.

    Watch out

    • Getting hit pushes your casts back and slows them down. If an enemy reaches you, Frost Shock slows it — step away, then cast.
    Shaman — EnhancementMeleeFirst-timer: MediumWeapon can hit extra times for free. Melee hits power up instant lightning.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Keep your weapon imbue up. Rockbiter Weapon early on; from level 30, Windfury Weapon — the one that makes your weapon hit extra times for free.
    2. Stormstrike. Once you learn it, this is your biggest weapon hit. Use it on cooldown.
    3. Flame Shock. A burn over time — cheap and always worth having on the enemy.
    4. Lightning Bolt. Your melee hits build up a talent (Maelstrom Weapon) that makes Lightning Bolt instant. When it's ready, fire it off — free damage.
    5. Earth Shock. Instant damage that also interrupts enemy spell casts. Shocks share one cooldown, so pick the right shock for the moment.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever your weapon imbues work alongside sharpening stones — use both.
    • Totem presets (level 20) let you drop your usual totems much faster.
    • Two-handed axes and maces are yours by default here — a slow two-hander with Windfury is the classic leveling route.

    Leveling tips

    • A slow weapon wins with Windfury: fewer, bigger swings mean bigger free extra hits. On gear: Strength and Agility.
    • Your melee hits power your spells and your spells support your melee — if you're only doing one half, you're playing half the spec.

    Watch out

    • Healing mid-fight empties the mana you need for shocks and costs you damage. Top yourself up AFTER the fight, and keep a totem down when fights will last.
    MageArcane · Fire · Frost — 3 damage options, tap to open⌄
    Mage — ArcaneRangedFirst-timer: MediumEach magic blast makes you stronger, but costs more mana (your spell energy).Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Arcane Blast. Your main nuke. Each cast makes the NEXT Arcane Blast stronger — but also costs more mana. That's the whole spec in one button.
    2. Arcane Missiles. A channeled stream of missiles. Steady, reliable damage when you need to mind your mana.
    3. Frostbolt. A cheap, safe filler — and it slows the enemy walking at you.
    4. Evocation. Channel to refill a big chunk of mana. Use it between fights or when you're safely out of reach.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Mana is your real health bar. Blast stacks feel amazing and drain you in seconds — the skill is knowing when to stop stacking and coast.
    • Conjure your own water and drink often. Running dry mid-fight means standing there doing nothing.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect above all — a bigger mana pool means more Arcane Blasts before you're empty.
    • A good rhythm while learning: a few Arcane Blasts, then Missiles until your mana looks healthy again.

    Watch out

    • Going all-out on every normal enemy leaves you drinking after every fight — or dead when an extra enemy joins. Pace the blasts: small fights get small spending.
    Mage — FireRangedFirst-timer: MediumCrits (extra-strong hits) speed up your next huge fireball, Pyroblast.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Fireball. Your main cast — big, slow, dependable fire damage.
    2. Scorch. A fast, cheap fire cast. Good for finishing enemies and while you're learning.
    3. Fire Blast. Instant — no cast time. Use it while moving or to finish something now.
    4. Pyroblast. Your huge fireball. Critical hits build Heating Up, which stacks and makes Pyroblast cast faster and faster — launch it when it's charged up.
    5. Flamestrike. For big groups: fire rains on an area. Only worth it on 3+ enemies.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Crits are your engine: every critical hit feeds Heating Up, and Heating Up feeds Pyroblast. The spec snowballs — the more crit you have, the more often the big one flies.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect first, and take critical-hit gear when you see it — it literally makes your best button happen more.
    • Drink between fights. Fire is thirsty.

    Watch out

    • Long cast times plus cloth armor: if an enemy reaches you, stop casting, Frost Nova it in place (yes, fire mages have Frost Nova), step back, and restart.
    Mage — FrostRangedFirst-timer: EasyFreezes enemies in place + slows them. Safest mage, good against groups.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Frostbolt. Your main cast — and it slows the enemy, so they take longer to reach you.
    2. Frost Nova. Freezes enemies around you in place. Cast it, then STEP BACK before you keep casting.
    3. Cone of Cold. Instant frost in a cone in front of you — good damage and a strong slow.
    4. Blizzard. For groups: ice rains on an area and slows everything in it (40% with the Improved Blizzard talent).
    5. Fire Blast. Instant finisher for enemies that almost died — every mage has it, frost included.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Fingers of Frost (talent): frozen enemies give your spells a big crit chance — when something is frozen, hit it with your hardest spell.
    • Freezes can break early when the frozen enemy takes damage, and bosses can't be frozen at all — in boss fights, your slows and Fingers of Frost do the work instead.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Polymorph is your best friend on multi-enemy pulls: turn one enemy into a sheep, fight the others, come back for it.
    • This is the safest mage, not the fastest — fights take a little longer and you almost never die. That's the trade, and it's a good one for a first character.

    Watch out

    • Don't stand still in melee range trading hits — your whole kit is built on distance. If something reaches you: Frost Nova, step back, keep casting.

    Warlock — AfflictionRangedFirst-timer: EasyLoads enemies with many slow damage spells. Their health drains over time.Tap to open the starter guide⌄

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Agony. Your strongest damage-over-time spell — it grows stronger the longer it stays on. In WoW Forever Agony is a 'Bane', which means it no longer blocks your Curses: you can run a Bane AND a Curse at the same time.
    2. Corruption. A second damage-over-time spell. Load it on right after Agony.
    3. Shadow Bolt. Your filler while the drains do their work.
    4. Drain Life. Channels health out of the enemy and into you — damage and healing in one button.
    5. Siphon Life. A talent: another drain that quietly heals you every few seconds while it ticks.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • In WoW Forever your damage-over-time spells can land critical hits — your drains hit harder than classic players remember.
    • Wrack is this tree's key talent, built around your Agony. The whole spec is: load drains, keep them rolling, stay alive.
    • Life Tap turns your health into mana, and your drains turn it back. That loop is why this warlock almost never has to stop.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Stamina — stamina is fuel for Life Tap, not just health.
    • Fear is your crowd control: one enemy runs away scared while you finish another. Practice it before you need it.

    Watch out

    • Drains kill slowly at the start of a fight. Don't pull three enemies at once until your drains are strong — one extra pull is how affliction warlocks die.

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Send your demon in first. Your Voidwalker holds the enemy's attention. Let it arrive before you cast anything.
    2. Corruption and Agony. Load your damage-over-time spells once the demon has the enemy.
    3. Shadow Bolt. Your filler damage between refreshes.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Stamina and Intellect. Your demon scales with your stats, so your gear is its gear.
    • Keep Soul Shards stocked — summoning demons costs them.

    Watch out

    • Your demon is tough, not immortal — pet durability was specifically tuned at launch. If it dies, everything turns on you: Fear the enemy, resummon, or run.

    • You can leave form and heal yourself at any time. That self-heal is the druid advantage: slower fights, almost no deaths.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Intellect and Spirit. Root-and-cast is your safest pattern: root, back up, Starfire.
    • Carry drinks — Balance is mana-hungry, and shapeshifting itself costs mana too.

    Watch out

    • If a fight goes wrong, don't die in owl form: shift to Bear Form for the armor, or Travel/Cat speed to run, THEN recover. New balance druids forget their forms are escape buttons.

    Your first buttons — in order

    1. Prowl, then open. Your stealth. Pounce stuns the enemy as you open; Ravage is the big-damage opener. Pick per fight.
    2. Claw. Your builder in cat form: spends energy, adds a combo point (up to 5, shown on the enemy).
    3. Shred. Your hardest builder — but only from BEHIND the enemy. Get behind whenever you can.
    4. Rip. Spend combo points on this finisher: a bleed that ticks over time. More points = a longer bleed. This is your best damage per energy.
    5. Ferocious Bite. The other finisher: instant burst. Use it when Rip is already ticking or to finish an enemy off.

    Good to know in your first hour

    • Cat form runs on energy and combo points, like a rogue — builders add points, finishers spend them, and waiting a beat for energy is normal, not a mistake.
    • Bear Form is your emergency button: huge armor and health. Frenzied Regeneration in bear form can turn rage into a massive heal.
    • Leader of the Pack (talent) raises your whole group's critical-hit chance — in a group, people are glad you're there.

    Leveling tips

    • On gear: Agility first — it powers cat damage and your dodging in bear too.
    • Bleed first, bite second: a fight that opens Rip → Shred → Ferocious Bite beats button-mashing every time.

    Watch out

    • Dumping all your energy the second you have it leaves you standing there auto-attacking. Pool your energy, spend it in bursts — and if you pull too much, Bear Form and run.

    🛡️ Tank & Healer Mini-Guides

    Not everyone has to deal damage. The 8 specs that tank and heal — shorter guides, same plain language. Checked against WoW Forever launch info — Nov 22, 2026.

    Tank & healer mini-guides — the other 8 specsTap to open⌄

    Why these exist. The quiz finds your damage character — but a group of five is one tank, one healer, and three damage. If you'd rather hold the enemies or keep everyone alive, these are your 8 options, in a shorter mini-guide format. Every tank and healer below is a different talent tree of a class the quiz already covers.

    Picking a tank. Warrior = the most tanking tools and the classic pick. Paladin = the easiest pack control, and in Forever it finally has a real taunt. Bear Druid = the biggest health pool and the simplest buttons — and the same character can be your cat damage spec.

    Picking a healer. Priest = the most versatile (two different healing styles). Shaman = the group healer, and the toughest in mail armor. Druid = heals over time, and the only healer who can also tank. Paladin = the strongest single-target healer, in plate.

    Dual Specialization. At level 40 you unlock a second talent set you can swap to. That's how most players cover these roles: level as damage, keep a tank or healer build as your second set for dungeons.

    Tanks — hold the enemiesWarrior — Protection (tank) · Paladin — Protection (tank) · Druid — Feral (Bear) (tank) — tap to open⌄
    Warrior — Protection (tank)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The classic tank: a shield, heavy plate, and the deepest toolbox for holding enemies' attention and surviving them.

    Defensive Stance. Your tanking stance — you generate much more threat and take less damage. Tanking happens in this stance.

    Taunt. Forces one enemy to attack you for a few seconds. Your rescue button when something runs at your healer.

    Sunder Armor. Stack this on tough enemies: each stack lowers their armor (so everyone deals more damage) and builds heavy threat for you.

    Shield Block + Revenge. Block an attack and Revenge lights up — a cheap, high-threat hit. This pair is your everyday threat engine.

    Shield Slam. The Protection tree's signature hit: big damage, big threat, and it can strip a magic buff off the enemy.

    Good to know. Rage is your fuel and you earn it by being hit — pulling first isn't just your job, it's how you power up. On gear: Stamina and armor first, and a good shield matters more than a fancy weapon.

    Watch out. You're the most gear-dependent tank. If your shield and armor fall behind your level, no amount of button skill covers it — take the defensive quest rewards.

    Paladin — Protection (tank)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The group-control tank — and the headline Forever change: after years of being the tank without a taunt, Protection Paladin finally has one. A real main tank now.

    Seal of Fury + Righteous Fury. Both learned at level 10 — turn both on. Your swings add Holy damage, and while you hold a shield, every hit also builds a small absorb shield on you. Righteous Fury makes all that Holy damage generate far more threat.

    Judgement — your taunt. With Seal of Fury active, Judgement taunts the target for 3 seconds, from up to 10 yards away. Judgement no longer consumes your Seal, so the taunt is back every few seconds.

    Consecration. From level 20: holy fire on the ground under a pack. This is your glue for holding several enemies at once.

    Holy Shield. A Protection talent that raises your block and hurts attackers when you block — keep it running in every fight.

    Good to know. Blocking restores your mana (Shield Specialization) — as a paladin tank, being hit is literally your fuel supply. Your blessings and auras cover the whole raid in Forever, so groups love having you even beyond your tanking.

    Watch out. You still have no true interrupt: Hammer of Justice (a stun) is your only way to stop a cast, and it's slow to come back. And since Judgement is both damage and taunt, don't fire it carelessly the moment before you'll need to grab an enemy.

    Druid — Feral (Bear) (tank)Tap to open⌄

    The job. Tanking as a bear: no shield, no block — instead a huge health pool and thick armor from Bear Form. The simplest tank buttons in the game.

    Bear Form. Your tank shape: armor and health jump way up. (Dire Bear Form at level 40 is the same idea, bigger.) You do everything below in this form.

    Growl. Your taunt — forces one enemy to attack you. Same rescue job as the warrior's.

    Maul. Your main hit: it powers up your next swing for heavy damage and threat. Most of your rage goes here.

    Swipe. Hits up to 3 enemies in front of you — your tool for holding a whole pack at once.

    Demoralizing Roar. Lowers the attack power of every enemy around you. Less damage coming in, for the whole fight — use it on every pack.

    Good to know. Forever's launch tuning more than doubled bear threat (the bear threat bonus went from +50% to +115%) — holding packs is far better than old Classic bear. Frenzied Regeneration turns your rage into health when a fight goes bad. On gear: Stamina above everything.

    Watch out. Your healing and utility spells need you OUT of bear form — shifting out mid-fight to heal usually gets someone killed. Trust your healer, stay bear.

    Healers — keep everyone alivePaladin — Holy (healer) · Priest — Discipline (healer) · Priest — Holy (healer) · Shaman — Restoration (healer) · Druid — Restoration (healer) — tap to open⌄
    Paladin — Holy (healer)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The tank's best friend: the strongest single-target healing in the game, delivered from inside plate armor. You keep one person alive through almost anything.

    Flash of Light. Fast and cheap — your everyday heal. Most of your button presses in a dungeon are this.

    Holy Light. The big, slow heal for heavy damage. Use it when Flash of Light can't keep up.

    Holy Shock. In Forever it's on a 10-second cooldown: an instant heal for emergencies (it can also hurt an enemy). Your only heal with no cast time — save it for the scary moments.

    Blessings. Your group buffs — put the right blessing on each person. In Forever, blessings last an hour, so this is a once-per-run job, not constant upkeep.

    Good to know. You wear plate and can stand near the fight, so you're the hardest healer to kill. Healing gear in Forever also gives you some spell damage (a third of your bonus healing), so a Holy paladin can still quest solo — slowly, but safely.

    Watch out. You have almost no group healing: when everyone takes damage at once, you still heal one person at a time. Triage — tank first, then you, then the rest.

    Priest — Discipline (healer)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The shield healer: instead of repairing damage, you prevent it. Absorb shields eat hits before health bars move. The thinking player's healer.

    Power Word: Shield. Your signature: an absorb shield on an ally that soaks damage before it lands. Shield the tank before pulls; shield anyone about to take a hit.

    Penance. New in Forever: a channeled spell that fires pulses — healing an ally, or damaging an enemy. Your best single-target heal once you have the talent.

    Flash Heal. Your fast direct heal, for when a shield wasn't enough or wasn't up.

    Renew. A heal over time — keep it rolling on the tank between shields.

    Good to know. Divine Aegis turns your critical heals into extra absorb shields, so prevention stacks on prevention. One limit to learn: a freshly shielded player carries Weakened Soul and can't be shielded again for a few seconds — rotate your shields around the group.

    Watch out. Shields do nothing if they land after the damage. Discipline is played a few seconds in the future: watch what the enemy is about to do, not what it just did.

    Priest — Holy (healer)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The all-rounder healer: the biggest healing toolbox in the game, with an answer for every shape of damage. If you want 'the healer' in its classic form, this is it.

    Greater Heal. Your big single-target heal — slow, expensive, and exactly what a hurt tank needs.

    Flash Heal. The fast emergency heal. Forever bonus (Holy Binding): Flash Heal on someone else also heals YOU for half as much — healing others keeps you alive too.

    Renew. Heal over time. Keep it on the tank at all times; sprinkle it on anyone taking steady damage.

    Prayer of Mending. Holy's new capstone in Forever: put it on an ally, and when they take damage it heals them and jumps to the next hurt teammate. One cast, several heals.

    Prayer of Healing. Your group heal — heals your whole party at once, slowly. For the moments when everything goes wrong together.

    Good to know. Priests are the only class with two full healing styles (Discipline's shields and Holy's direct healing) — Dual Spec at 40 lets you keep both. On gear: Intellect and Spirit keep your mana going.

    Watch out. Cloth armor and few escapes: you're the squishiest healer. Positioning is your defense — stand at the back, and if something reaches you, run toward your tank.

    Shaman — Restoration (healer)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The group healer: your signature heal bounces between teammates, and your totems quietly power the whole group. Also the toughest healer — mail armor and a shield.

    Chain Heal. Your signature: a heal that jumps to nearby allies, healing several people with one cast. The best group heal in the game when the group stands together.

    Healing Wave. Your big single-target heal for the tank.

    Lesser Healing Wave. The fast, small heal for emergencies.

    Riptide. Restoration's 31-point talent in Forever: an instant heal that keeps healing over time — and makes your next Chain Heal on that target stronger. Riptide the tank, then Chain Heal off them.

    Totems. Drop them where the group stands. Mana Tide Totem is the famous one: a mana battery for every caster in your group.

    Good to know. Water Shield keeps your own mana refilling while you heal. And like every Forever healer, your healing gear also grants some spell damage, so questing between dungeons isn't painful.

    Watch out. Totems only help people standing near them — when the group moves, your totems don't. Re-dropping them in the right spot is a real part of the job.

    Druid — Restoration (healer)Tap to open⌄

    The job. The heal-over-time healer: you cover the group in healing that ticks steadily, before and while damage lands. Calm, planned healing — and the only healer who can also be the tank.

    Rejuvenation. Your main heal over time — instant to cast. Keep it on anyone taking damage; it's the heartbeat of the spec.

    Regrowth. A direct heal plus a heal over time in one cast, with a high critical chance. Your mid-size answer.

    Healing Touch. The big, slow heal for heavy damage — your 'someone is about to die' button among the cast-time heals.

    Swiftmend. A talent: instantly converts your own heal-over-time already on a target into a burst heal. It needs YOUR Rejuvenation or Regrowth ticking on them first — plan one step ahead.

    Wild Growth. Restoration's new capstone in Forever: a heal over time on your whole party at once. Your answer to group-wide damage.

    Good to know. Your heals work ahead of time: the winning habit is covering the tank in heals BEFORE the pull starts. And your forms are escape buttons — Travel Form to catch up, Bear Form to survive a mistake.

    Watch out. Heals over time are slow by nature: a teammate at 10% health needs Swiftmend or Healing Touch right now, not another Rejuvenation. Also, your only resurrection is Rebirth — a battle resurrection on a long cooldown — so preventing deaths matters more for you than for other healers.

    Start playing togetherPlay together · Making your first character · Playing together from level 1 — tap to open⌄
    Play togetherTap to open⌄

    WoW Forever has no realm list — you pick a ruleset (start on Normal), and everyone on that ruleset shares one world. To group up you must be on the same ruleset AND the same faction (Alliance or Horde) as your friends. Decide together before anyone creates a character.

    Making your first characterTap to open⌄

    Your race decides your faction. Alliance races: Human, Dwarf, Night Elf, Gnome. Horde races: Orc, Troll, Tauren, Undead (a human who died and came back — they're on the Horde side here). You can only group with your own faction, so this is the choice to make together.

    Race is the small choice: each race gets a couple of minor special abilities, and that's mostly it. Class is the big choice — it decides how you fight for the entire game. If you're stuck between two races, pick the one you like looking at. You'll be staring at this character for a long time.

    Your name has to be unique on your ruleset. Pick something you won't be embarrassed to see in chat for the next year.

    Playing together from level 1Tap to open⌄

    Invite your friends into a party (a group of up to 5). Party members who are near each other share credit for quest kills — if your friend kills the wolf the quest sent you after, it counts for you too.

    Gathering quests are the exception: if the quest wants 8 wolf pelts, each of you needs your own 8 drops. Expect gathering quests to take the group a little longer — that's normal, not a bug.

    Each race starts in its own starting zone, so a Human and a Night Elf begin on opposite sides of the world. Don't panic: by around level 10–12 the starting zones feed into shared areas, and meeting up gets easy. Until then, everyone levels in their own zone.

    You can share most quests from your quest log with party members who are eligible — when you pick up a new quest, share it before you set off.

    Your characterTalents start at level 10 · Stats in plain language · Gear — colors, weapons, and armor · Trainers — your abilities aren't automatic — tap to open⌄
    Talents start at level 10Tap to open⌄

    You earn talent points as you level — 51 by level 60 — and spend them in your class's three trees. Your 'spec' is simply the tree you spend the most in. A class trainer in any major city can reset them, and at level 40 you unlock a second talent set you can swap to.

    Stats in plain languageTap to open⌄

    Strength — makes melee hits harder. The main stat for warriors and paladins, useful for anyone who swings a weapon.

    Agility — faster critical hits (extra-strong hits), more dodging, a little armor. The main stat for hunters and rogues, and for druids in cat form.

    Intellect — a bigger mana pool (mana is your spell energy), and your spells land critical hits slightly more often. The main stat for mages, priests, and warlocks.

    Spirit — faster health and mana regeneration when you're NOT in combat. It shortens your eating and drinking breaks, which quietly speeds up leveling.

    Stamina — more health. Everyone wants some; it's what keeps a bad pull from killing you.

    You don't need to memorize this for every class: each starter guide names the two stats that matter for that character. Gear whose main stat isn't yours is sell-it gear, no matter how shiny it looks.

    Gear — colors, weapons, and armorTap to open⌄

    Item names come in colors. Grey = junk, sell it to any vendor. White = plain but usable. Green = has bonus stats, your normal leveling gear. Blue = rare, a real upgrade. Purple = epic, the best tier — you won't see much of it while leveling, and that's fine.

    Two words on gear matter. 'Binds on pickup' means once you pick it up, it's yours forever — you can never trade or sell it to another player. 'Binds on equip' means you can trade or sell it until the moment you wear it.

    For melee classes and hunters, a better weapon is a bigger upgrade than any armor piece — your damage numbers come straight from it. Take weapon quest rewards first.

    Armor comes in four weights: cloth (lightest), leather, mail, plate (heaviest). Wear the heaviest type your class can use — warriors and paladins start in mail, hunters and shamans in leather, and at level 40 those classes can train the next weight up.

    Trainers — your abilities aren't automaticTap to open⌄

    Leveling up does NOT hand you your new spells. Every couple of levels, visit your class trainer in a town or capital city and BUY your new abilities — and the stronger ranks of the ones you already have. Forgetting this is the #1 reason new players feel mysteriously weak at level 15.

    The same trainer can reset your talent points if you want to change your build. It costs gold, and the price goes up each time you do it, so experiment early while it's cheap.

    Professions have trainers too — you learn each job, and its recipes and patterns, from them. Your class trainer is usually near other trainers in the same city district.

    Combat foundations — how WoW actually worksStopping enemy spells — interrupts · Aggro — who the enemy attacks · Crowd control — taking enemies out of the fight · Your other buttons — staying alive · Where you stand — positioning · Words you'll hear — quick translations · Out in the world — Forever systems — tap to open⌄
    Stopping enemy spells — interruptsTap to open⌄

    Casting and cast bars. When an enemy uses a big spell, a bar appears and fills up. If it finishes, the spell happens. While it fills, the enemy can be stopped.

    Interrupt. An ability that cancels the spell being cast AND locks that kind of spell for a few seconds. Yours: Rogue — Kick. Warrior — Pummel. Mage — Counterspell. Shaman — Earth Shock. A warlock's Felhunter pet has one too (Spell Lock). Shadow priests can take the Silence talent. Hunters, druids and paladins have NO true interrupt — read the next line.

    Stuns and silences. The backup plan: a stunned or silenced enemy can't cast either. Paladin — Hammer of Justice. Rogue — Cheap Shot / Kidney Shot. Druid — Bash (in Bear Form). Hunter — Intimidation (Beast Mastery pet stun) or Scatter Shot (Marksmanship). If your class can't interrupt, stunning and killing the caster fast is your job.

    What to interrupt first. Enemy HEALS — an interrupted heal is a dead enemy. Then fears and mind controls (they turn your own side against you), then big damage spells aimed at your tank. A normal small spell usually isn't worth it.

    If nobody interrupts. Healers heal forever, summoners summon friends, and fear casters scatter your group. In dungeons, 'we died because nothing got interrupted' is one of the most common wipe causes there is.

    Aggro — who the enemy attacksTap to open⌄

    Threat (aggro). Every enemy keeps a mental list of who has annoyed it most — damage and healing both count. Whoever is on top gets attacked. 'Having aggro' means it's coming for you.

    The tank's job. Staying on top of that list on purpose, with armor and a shield to survive it. One player tanks, everyone else's job includes NOT climbing above them.

    Taunt. The tank's rescue button: forces an enemy to attack the tank for a few seconds, no matter what the list said. If something runs past the tank at your healer, the tank taunts it back.

    Your rules as damage. Let the tank hit the enemy a few times BEFORE you go all-out. Attack the same enemy the tank is attacking. If an enemy does chase you, run TOWARD the tank (so they can take it off you) — never away, and never keep hitting it.

    Pet tanks. Same rules with a pet: let your pet hit first and build its lead, then shoot. If you open with your biggest shot, the enemy skips the pet and comes straight for you.

    Crowd control — taking enemies out of the fightTap to open⌄

    Crowd control (CC). Abilities that disable an enemy without killing it, so your group fights one enemy at a time instead of four: a mage's Polymorph turns one into a sheep, a rogue's Sap knocks one out before the fight, a hunter's Freezing Trap freezes one, roots pin one in place, fear sends one running.

    CC breaks. Sheep, traps and Sap BREAK the moment that enemy takes damage. One careless area spell can wake everything up at once. Never hit a disabled enemy, and don't use area attacks near one.

    Target marks. Group leaders stick icons on enemies. Skull almost always means 'kill this one first'. Moon usually means 'mage, sheep this one'. If you're not sure what a mark means in your group, ask — it's a normal question.

    Focus fire. The flip side of CC: everyone attacks the SAME awake enemy until it's dead. Five half-dead enemies hit just as hard as five healthy ones — kills shrink the danger, damage spread everywhere doesn't.

    Your other buttons — staying aliveTap to open⌄

    Defensive cooldowns. Every class has one or two 'oh no' buttons (a paladin's bubble, a mage's Ice Block, a rogue's Vanish, a warrior's Shield Wall). Use them when a fight suddenly goes wrong or you're near 30% health. A defensive you never press saves nobody — they recharge.

    Potions and bandages. Health potions are instant and work mid-fight. Bandages (from the First Aid skill) heal a lot but break if you're hit — bandage when nothing is attacking you. Carry both; vendors and other players sell them.

    Cooldowns. Your strongest abilities can't be spammed — they recharge over seconds or minutes (that's the 'cooldown'). Big cooldowns are for big moments: hard enemies, extra pulls, bosses. Using one on a normal enemy then facing a boss without it is the classic trade-off.

    Food, drink and buffs. Eat and drink between fights — full health and mana, faster starts. Some food and spells give 'buffs': temporary stat boosts. In a group, share yours; someone will share theirs.

    Where you stand — positioningTap to open⌄

    Don't stand in it. Fire on the ground, poison pools, glowing circles: if it's under your feet and you didn't put it there, move. Most avoidable deaths in the game are just standing still in something bad.

    Front and back. Enemies mostly hit what's in front of them. Rogues and cat druids literally do more damage from BEHIND. When someone else has the enemy's attention, stand behind it — safer and stronger.

    Line of sight. Enemy casters can't cast through walls. Pull them around a corner and they have to walk to you — straight into your group. Tanks do this on purpose; don't stand out in the open undoing it.

    Runners. Many enemies try to flee at low health — and they run TO their friends, bringing them back. Slow them, stun them, or finish them fast. A runner nobody stopped is how a 2-enemy fight becomes a 6-enemy fight.

    Words you'll hear — quick translationsTap to open⌄

    Mob. Any computer-controlled enemy. (From old game slang — it's just 'a monster'.)

    Pull. Starting a fight with an enemy or group, usually one pack at a time.

    Add. An extra enemy that joins a fight already in progress. Usually bad news.

    Elite. A much stronger enemy with a gold dragon around its portrait. Elites are built for groups — solo players should walk around them.

    Buff / debuff. A helpful temporary effect on you (buff) or a harmful one on you or an enemy (debuff). Curses, poisons and diseases are debuffs some classes can remove ('dispel' / 'cleanse').

    DoT / HoT. Damage over Time / Healing over Time — effects that keep ticking after one button press.

    Proc. A random bonus effect triggering — 'it procced' means the lucky thing happened.

    Crit. A critical hit: an extra-strong hit (usually double damage) that happens by chance.

    Spec. Your specialization — the talent tree you put most points in. 'What's your spec?' means 'which of the three versions of your class are you?'

    Wipe. The whole group died. Everyone runs back together and tries again — normal, not a disaster.

    NPC. Any computer-controlled character — quest givers, vendors, trainers. Yellow name = neutral, red = will attack, green = friendly.

    Out in the world — Forever systemsTap to open⌄

    Hearthstone. A stone in your bag that teleports you back to one chosen inn (10-second cast, 1-hour cooldown). Talk to the innkeeper in each new quest town and set it there — it's your 'go home' button.

    Rested experience. Logging out inside an inn or a capital city builds up rested XP (a little every few hours, up to about a level and a half). While you have it, kills give DOUBLE experience. Log out in inns — it's free progress while you sleep.

    Campfires. A new WoW Forever system: around level 5 a quest teaches you Basic Campfire (from Cooking). Camps and campfires out in the world give rested XP and useful buffs, and other players can add to your fire. Worth learning early.

    Dying. You turn into a ghost and run back to your body to revive where you fell. A Spirit Healer can revive you instantly instead, but it damages your gear and — above level 10 — leaves you weak (resurrection sickness) for up to 10 minutes. Running back is almost always better.

    Repairs. Your gear wears out as you fight and especially when you die. Vendors repair it for coins — repair before dungeon runs, or your armor quietly breaks mid-fight.

    Trainers. New abilities aren't automatic: every couple of levels, visit your class trainer in a town or city and BUY your new spells and their stronger ranks. Forgetting this is why some new players feel mysteriously weak.

    Getting a group. There's no automatic dungeon queue in WoW Forever: groups form through the listing tool and chat, and you walk to the dungeon entrance together. Slow, but it's how you'll meet people.

    Your first mount. At level 40 you can learn riding and buy your first mount (about 100 gold) — much faster travel. It's the big purchase of your leveling life: don't blow all your gold at level 30.

    Leveling & the worldThe leveling loop · Staying alive · Hearthstone, rested XP, and campfires · Travel — and your first mount at 40 · Money basics · Dying — and getting back — tap to open⌄
    The leveling loopTap to open⌄

    Quest, kill one enemy at a time at first, loot everything, and sell junk to vendors. Quest rewards are your main gear upgrades — for melee and hunters, a weapon upgrade beats everything else.

    Staying aliveTap to open⌄

    Eat food and drink water between fights to refill health and mana — everyone does, it's not a weakness. Pulling one extra enemy is the #1 cause of death for new players. Pull small until a fight feels boring.

    Hearthstone, rested XP, and campfiresTap to open⌄

    Your Hearthstone is a stone in your bag that teleports you back to one chosen inn (10-second cast, then a 1-hour cooldown before you can use it again). Talk to the innkeeper in every new quest town and set your Hearthstone there — it's your 'go home' button at the end of a session.

    Logging out inside an inn or a capital city builds up rested XP — a bonus that makes kills worth DOUBLE experience until it runs out. It builds a little every few hours, up to about a level and a half. Logging out in an inn is free progress while you sleep.

    Campfires are new in WoW Forever: around level 5 a quest teaches you Basic Campfire, from the Cooking skill. Camps and campfires out in the world also give rested XP and useful buffs, and other players can add to your fire — worth learning early.

    Travel — and your first mount at 40Tap to open⌄

    At the start, you walk. Everywhere. That's normal — the world is built for it, and quests chain from town to town so you're rarely walking without a purpose.

    Flight paths are your fast travel: talk to the flight master in each new town to unlock that stop, and from then on you can pay a few coins to fly between any two stops you've unlocked. Unlock every flight master you pass — future you says thanks.

    Boats (Alliance) and zeppelins (Horde) connect the continents and are free — just stand on board and wait for the ride. Ask a city guard for directions to the docks or the zeppelin tower.

    At level 40 you can learn riding and buy your first mount — about 100 gold all-in — and travel speed jumps for good. It's the big purchase of your leveling life: start saving in your 30s. A much faster epic mount exists at level 60, at around 1,000 gold — that's a long-term goal, not something to stress about now.

    Money basicsTap to open⌄

    Loot everything and sell the grey junk to any vendor. It feels like pennies because it is — but pennies are exactly how the level-40 mount gets paid for.

    Don't buy gear from vendors. Vendor weapons and armor are overpriced and weak; quest rewards and enemy drops are your gear. The one early purchase that IS worth it: bigger bags. More bag space means fewer town trips and less loot left behind.

    Capital cities have an Auction House, where players buy and sell from each other. While leveling, your main use for it is selling — materials you gather (ore, herbs, leather, cloth) are worth real money to other players, often more than you'd expect.

    Repair your gear at vendors before it breaks, and keep a small gold cushion. Running completely dry is the only real money trap at low levels.

    Dying — and getting backTap to open⌄

    When you die, you become a ghost at the nearest graveyard, and you run back to your body to come back to life where you fell. The run is annoying on purpose — it's the game's main punishment for dying — but it costs you nothing but time.

    A Spirit Healer at the graveyard can revive you instantly instead, but it damages your gear and — above level 10 — leaves you weakened (resurrection sickness: your stats and damage drop hard) for up to 10 minutes. Running back is almost always the better deal.

    Your gear also takes a little damage every time you die, so deaths quietly cost repair money too. One more reason the guides keep saying it: pull one enemy at a time.

    Groups & dungeonsIn a dungeon group · Loot manners — Need, Greed, Pass — tap to open⌄
    In a dungeon groupTap to open⌄

    Five players: one tank (holds the enemies' attention), one healer, three damage. Your job as damage: let the tank pull first, then attack the same enemy the tank is fighting. Don't run ahead, and don't hit enemies nobody else is fighting.

    Loot manners — Need, Greed, PassTap to open⌄

    When something good drops in a group, a window pops up and everyone picks one: Need, Greed, or Pass. Players who pick Need roll first; if nobody needs it, the Greed players roll; Pass means 'not for me.'

    Need = it's an upgrade for the character you're playing right now, and you'll equip it. Greed = you'd sell it, use it on a different character, or just want the money. Pass = you don't want it at all.

    The one rule that keeps groups friendly: only Need on gear for the spec you're playing in that run. If you want something for a different build, say so in chat and ask — most groups say yes, and asking costs you nothing.

    Not sure? Greed is always safe. Nobody ever got kicked for greeding; people absolutely get remembered for needing everything.

    Rulesets & PvPRulesets & PvP — fighting other players — tap to open⌄
    Rulesets & PvP — fighting other playersTap to open⌄

    Forever's worlds come in rulesets instead of separate realms. On a <b>Normal</b> ruleset, other players can only fight you if you choose to join in. On a <b>PvP</b> ruleset, players from the enemy faction can attack you out in the world — and you can attack them. Your whole group must pick the SAME ruleset to play together (see 'Play together' above), so decide this before anyone creates a character.

    <b>Which should new players pick?</b> Normal, for a first group. Learning the game is challenge enough without enemy players camping your quest area. A PvP ruleset is for players who WANT that open-world danger as part of the game — some people love it, but go in knowing that's the deal.

    <b>How world PvP works.</b> In contested and enemy territory, an enemy-faction player can attack you at any time — and near their towns, their guards will join in. Your own starting zones are safe ground: enemies can't easily reach you there. If you do get jumped, treat it like a bad pull: run toward your own town or guards, use your escape buttons, or fight back.

    <b>Fights you choose.</b> On ANY ruleset you can duel a friend (a friendly one-on-one that stops before anyone dies), and battlegrounds offer organized team-vs-team PvP matches with objectives like capture-the-flag — player-vs-player on purpose, with even teams, instead of ambushes.

    🛠️ WoW Forever Professions

    Jobs your character learns alongside fighting: gathering raw materials, crafting gear and potions, and three side skills everyone gets. You pick TWO primary professions — this section helps you pick well. Checked against WoW Forever launch info — Nov 15, 2026.

    Start hereHow professions work — read this first · The beginner default — if you don't want to think about it — tap to open⌄
    How professions work — read this firstTap to open⌄

    The two-slot rule. Every character can learn TWO primary professions (any mix of gathering and crafting) AND all three secondary professions (Cooking, First Aid, Fishing). Secondary ones never use a primary slot — learn all three, always.

    Skill and ranks. Each profession runs from skill 1 to 300. Trainers promote you through four ranks as you grow: Apprentice (to 75), Journeyman (to 150), Expert (to 225), Artisan (to 300). Recipes show in colors: orange recipes almost always raise your skill, yellow usually, green sometimes, grey never.

    Learning and unlearning. You learn professions — and most recipes — from trainers in towns and capital cities. You can drop a profession at any time to make room for another, but its skill resets to zero: if you ever relearn it, you start over. Choose like you mean it.

    A head start. Each starting zone hides an early quest item that teaches you one gathering profession (Herbalism, Mining, or Skinning) — watch your bags in your first levels.

    New in Forever. Professions got bigger here: 600+ new recipes, advanced Blueprint recipes that drop from dungeon bosses, crafted food with an XP bonus, and the Camping system — from skill 20, most professions can place a Camp Feature at a campfire that buffs everyone sitting there. Crafting professions also earn account-wide Legacy Points at skill 150, 225, and 300 — one more reason gathering-only characters eventually want a craft.

    The beginner default — if you don't want to think about itTap to open⌄

    The answer. Take Skinning + Herbalism (or Mining) while you level. Sell everything you gather on the Auction House. Decide on crafting later, when you're rich or level 60.

    Why it works. Gathering costs nothing — no recipes, no materials, no trainer bills — and launch economies pay top prices for raw materials because every crafter needs them. Your two gathering professions quietly pay for your level-40 mount, which is the purchase that actually matters.

    When to switch. At 60 (or once the mount is bought), drop one gathering profession for the craft that fits your class — Alchemy for potions, your armor craft to gear yourself — and keep the gathering profession that feeds it. Dropping is free; only the dropped skill is lost.

    The one exception. Cloth casters (Mage, Priest, Warlock): Tailoring is so cheap to level — your materials drop from normal enemies — that Tailoring + one gathering profession is an equally good default for you.

    Gathering — free materials, sell or feed a craftHerbalism · Mining · Skinning — tap to open⌄
    HerbalismTap to open⌄

    What it is. You pick herbs growing in the wild — flowers and plants shown on your mini-map once you turn on Find Herbs. Herbs are the main ingredient for Alchemy, and some feed First Aid's potions.

    What you actually get. A steady stream of sellable herbs, plus the herbs to power an Alchemist friend (or yourself). At a campfire, Herbalism's Camp Feature is the Incense Candle, which raises Intellect for campers.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing — no tools, no recipes to buy. You gather while you quest, so it levels itself. One of the two easiest professions in the game.

    Pairs with. Alchemy (it eats your herbs), or Skinning/Mining for a double-gathering money setup.

    Take it if. You want income with zero effort, or anyone in your group is an Alchemist.

    Beginner verdict. Top-tier first pick. Take it and never regret it.

    MiningTap to open⌄

    What it is. You dig ore, stone, and the occasional gem out of rock veins, using a Mining Pick. You can also smelt ore into metal bars, which is how Blacksmithing and Engineering actually consume it.

    What you actually get. Ore and bars that always sell — smiths and engineers need a constant supply — plus stone used for sharpening stones and explosives.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing but the pick. Veins cluster around hills, caves, and zone edges, so it takes slightly more detouring than Herbalism.

    Pairs with. Blacksmithing or Engineering (both eat bars), or Herbalism for the classic double-gathering money pair.

    Take it if. You want the most reliable Auction House income, or you're feeding a smith/engineer.

    Beginner verdict. Top-tier first pick, tied with Herbalism. Mining + Herbalism is the launch money standard.

    SkinningTap to open⌄

    What it is. After a beast dies, you skin its corpse with a Skinning Knife and take its leather and hide. Leather feeds Leatherworking; everything also sells.

    What you actually get. Leather from the animals you're already killing for quests — income that happens by itself, with no nodes to hunt down.

    Cost & effort. Costs nothing but the knife. The only catch: it only works on beasts, so it shines in beast-heavy zones and does nothing in, say, an undead crypt.

    Pairs with. Leatherworking (it eats your leather), or Herbalism/Mining for double gathering.

    Take it if. Your class wears leather (Rogue, Druid) or you just want the laziest money in the game.

    Beginner verdict. The easiest money profession while leveling. Skin everything you kill.

    Crafting — turn materials into gear and powerAlchemy · Blacksmithing · Enchanting · Engineering · Leatherworking · Tailoring — tap to open⌄
    AlchemyTap to open⌄

    What it is. You brew herbs into potions (instant effects — healing, mana, invisibility), elixirs (stat boosts that last through a fight), and at high skill, flasks (powerful boosts that persist through death) and transmutations (turning one material into another, on a cooldown). Note: in Forever, the standard Healing Potions moved to First Aid — Alchemy's strength is buffs, mana, and utility.

    What you actually get. The consumables every dungeon and raid group burns through constantly — which means permanent demand for what you make. At a campfire, Alchemy's Camp Feature is the Mana Well, restoring mana to campers.

    Cost & effort. Moderate: recipes from trainers plus some rare ones from vendors and drops. Needs a constant herb supply — bring Herbalism or a Herbalist friend.

    Pairs with. Herbalism. This is the most natural pair in the game.

    Take it if. You want to be the group's potion supply, or you like steady Auction House income from things people consume and rebuy.

    Beginner verdict. The most group-friendly crafting profession, and the safest crafting pick for a new player.

    BlacksmithingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You forge metal bars into mail and plate armor and most melee weapons, plus sharpening stones and weightstones that temporarily boost a weapon. You'll need an anvil (found in every town) for most recipes.

    What you actually get. Gear for warriors and paladins — the two classes that can't easily gear themselves any other way — including some of the best pre-raid weapons and armor at 60.

    Cost & effort. Expensive in materials: it eats ore and bars constantly. Without Mining (or a rich alt), you'll buy your materials and feel it.

    Pairs with. Mining. Non-negotiable for a first character.

    Take it if. You're a Warrior or Paladin who wants to wear your own work, or your group's plate wearers need a smith.

    Beginner verdict. Great on a Warrior/Paladin with Mining. Painful without the gathering half.

    EnchantingTap to open⌄

    What it is. Two jobs in one: you DISENCHANT magic items (green quality or better) and break them down into dusts, essences, and shards — then you spend those materials putting permanent stat boosts (enchants) on weapons and armor: yours, your group's, anyone's.

    What you actually get. The only way to upgrade gear you already own, and a service other players pay well for at 60. Every enchant you do consumes materials you got by destroying items.

    Cost & effort. The most expensive profession to level: every skill point comes from items you could have sold. New players feel this one in the wallet.

    Pairs with. Tailoring — the classic self-sufficient duo: tailors craft green items from dropped cloth, and you disenchant them for materials. No gathering needed.

    Take it if. You like being useful to everyone at 60 and don't mind a slower, pricier road getting there.

    Beginner verdict. Powerful but pricey. On a first character, consider taking it as your SECOND character's job — or pair it with Tailoring and be patient.

    EngineeringTap to open⌄

    What it is. You build gadgets: bombs and grenades, target dummies, scopes for guns and bows, jumper cables that can restart a dead player's heart (sometimes), and goggles only engineers can wear. Many items only work for engineers.

    What you actually get. Fun and power that no other profession gives — extra stuns, burst damage, and utility that shines in PvP and saves dungeon runs. Also the game's best toys.

    Cost & effort. Expensive and gloriously impractical: it eats ore, stone, and odd parts, and most of what you make can't be sold to non-engineers. You do this one for yourself.

    Pairs with. Mining — it feeds on bars and stone.

    Take it if. You want your character to feel different from everyone else's, and you accept that it's a gold sink, not a gold source.

    Beginner verdict. The fun pick, not the rich pick. Wonderful second profession once your mount is paid for.

    LeatherworkingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You turn leather and hides into leather armor (Rogues, Druids) and mail armor (Hunters, Shamans), plus armor kits that add a small permanent armor boost to a piece of gear.

    What you actually get. A full self-gearing path for four classes, and armor kits everyone can use. Forever massively expanded its recipe list — it's a deeper profession here than it was in old Classic.

    Cost & effort. Moderate: leather is cheap if you skin it yourself, pricey if you don't. Some recipes also want thread and dyes from vendors.

    Pairs with. Skinning. Leather is the whole diet.

    Take it if. You play Rogue, Druid, Hunter, or Shaman and want to wear what you make.

    Beginner verdict. Solid with Skinning on the right class. Skip it on cloth and plate classes.

    TailoringTap to open⌄

    What it is. You sew cloth — which drops from humanoid enemies everywhere — into cloth armor for Mages, Priests, and Warlocks, shirts, and BAGS. Bags are the headline: bigger bags are every player's first real purchase, and tailors make them.

    What you actually get. Gear for your cloth caster with NO gathering profession needed (the world drops your materials), plus a product — bags — that sells at every level, forever.

    Cost & effort. The cheapest crafting profession to level, because cloth falls out of normal questing. Some high-end patterns drop from enemies or come from vendors.

    Pairs with. Enchanting (make greens, disenchant them) — or any gathering profession, since Tailoring doesn't need the slot.

    Take it if. You're a Mage, Priest, or Warlock — or you just want the bag business.

    Beginner verdict. The friendliest crafting profession for a first character. Cloth class? Take it.

    Secondary — everyone learns all threeCooking · First Aid · Fishing — tap to open⌄
    CookingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You cook raw meat and fish into food that restores health faster — and good food adds a 'Well Fed' stat buff for 15+ minutes. In Forever, crafted food also grants a small XP bonus, so eating before a quest run literally levels you faster.

    What you actually get. Cheaper, stronger recovery than vendor food, real stat buffs, and the campfire itself: Cooking's Basic Campfire is what creates a campsite where everyone's Camp Features work.

    Cost & effort. Nearly free: your materials are the meat from beasts you already kill. Learn it at level 5-ish and keep it current — it's the first profession a new Forever player touches.

    Pairs with. Fishing (fish are prime cooking ingredients) and every beast you ever kill.

    Take it if. Everyone. It's secondary — it doesn't use one of your two primary slots. There is no reason not to.

    Beginner verdict. Mandatory. Learn it on day one.

    First AidTap to open⌄

    What it is. You turn cloth into bandages that heal you mid-adventure without mana or a healer — bandage between fights, or even during a fight if nothing is hitting you. Forever expanded it: First Aid now also crafts the standard Healing Potions and cures for poison and disease.

    What you actually get. Self-healing for the classes that have none (Warrior, Rogue, Hunter, Mage) and an emergency heal for everyone else. In dungeons, a bandage can save a run when the healer is out of mana.

    Cost & effort. Nearly free: cloth is everywhere. It competes with Tailoring for cloth — if you're both, bandages for leveling, sewing for selling.

    Pairs with. Everything. It's secondary — no primary slot used.

    Take it if. Everyone, but it's life-changing for Warriors and Rogues especially.

    Beginner verdict. Mandatory. The closest thing professions have to a cheat code for staying alive.

    FishingTap to open⌄

    What it is. You cast a line into any water with a Fishing Pole and catch fish — ingredients for Cooking and Alchemy — plus the occasional locked box, useful item, or junk you'll laugh at.

    What you actually get. Cooking ingredients you'd otherwise buy, some fish that Alchemists pay well for, and the most relaxing money in the game. At a campfire, Fishing's Camp Feature is the Fishing Bowl.

    Cost & effort. Free, slow, and proud of it. Skill rises by catching, and higher-level waters need higher skill (and sometimes a lure).

    Pairs with. Cooking — catch it, cook it, eat the buff.

    Take it if. You like a calm grind, or your Cooking keeps running out of fish.

    Beginner verdict. Optional but charming. Learn it when you want a break from killing things.

    How to read these routesTap to open⌄

    The one rule. Craft whatever is ORANGE in your recipe list — orange means a guaranteed skill point. Yellow usually gives one, green sometimes, grey never. Every route below is just 'the cheapest orange thing' at each step, so if Forever's new recipes give you a cheaper orange option, take it — the route still works.

    Gather first, craft second. For crafting professions, gather (or buy) the materials for a whole bracket BEFORE you start crafting it. Nothing wastes gold like crafting in ones and twos.

    Trainers gate you. You can't pass 75 / 150 / 225 / 300 without visiting a trainer for the next rank (Apprentice / Journeyman / Expert / Artisan) — and the last two ranks for some professions live with specific trainers out in the world. The route cards flag the special ones.

    Herbalism route — where to pick, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Your starting zone plus the next one over: Peacebloom, Silverleaf, Earthroot. Turn on Find Herbs and pick everything while you quest — this bracket levels itself.

    75–150. Hillsbrad Foothills, the Wetlands, and Ashenvale: Briarthorn, Bruiseweed, Kingsblood, Wild Steelbloom. Mageroyal grows near water and ruins.

    150–225. The Hinterlands, Feralas, and Felwood: Fadeleaf, Goldthorn, Khadgar's Whisker, then Purple Lotus and Sungrass as you push past 200.

    225–300. Felwood and the Eastern Plaguelands for Sungrass and Plaguebloom, Winterspring for Icecap and Mountain Silversage — the two most valuable herbs in the game. If it glows on your mini-map in a level-55+ zone, pick it.

    Mining route — where to dig, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Copper in any starting zone — Dun Morogh, Durotar, and Elwynn are full of it. Smelt everything; smelting gives skill early on.

    75–150. Tin (smelt Copper + Tin into Bronze for skill, too) in the Wetlands, Ashenvale, and Thousand Needles. Iron starts appearing at the top of this bracket in Hillsbrad and the Badlands edges.

    150–225. Iron in the Badlands and Arathi Highlands, then Mithril in Tanaris, the Hinterlands, and the Searing Gorge. Gold and Truesilver veins are bonuses, not the plan.

    225–300. Thorium: Un'Goro Crater, Winterspring, the Eastern Plaguelands, and Silithus. Rich Thorium veins carry the last stretch. Keep a route looping a zone's edges and caves — veins cluster there.

    Skinning route — where to hunt, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Skin every beast you kill while questing your starting zones: Light Leather and Ruined Scraps. Skinning levels off your normal leveling — never grind it separately.

    75–150. Medium Leather country: the Wetlands, Hillsbrad, and Ashenvale beasts. Raptors, wolves, and turtles are your best friends.

    150–225. Heavy Leather in the Hinterlands, Feralas, and Tanaris. Thick Leather starts appearing near 225 in Tanaris and the Plaguelands edges.

    225–300. Thick and Rugged Leather: Un'Goro's dinosaurs, Winterspring's chimaeras and bears, and the Plaguelands' plagued beasts. The last points are the slowest — skin while you quest at 60.

    Alchemy route — what to brew, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    Forever twist first. In Forever, the standard Healing Potions belong to First Aid — so the classic 'brew healing potions to 150' route doesn't exist here. This route runs on mana potions and elixirs instead.

    1–75. Minor Mana Potions (Mageroyal + Silverleaf) as far as they stay orange, then Elixir of Minor Defense to finish the bracket.

    75–150. Lesser Mana Potions (Mageroyal + Stranglekelp) and Elixirs of Wisdom (Mageroyal + Briarthorn). Stranglekelp grows underwater along coasts — swim for it.

    150–225. Elixir of Agility (Stranglekelp + Goldthorn), then Elixir of Greater Intellect and mana potions of the next size as they turn orange. Goldthorn is the bottleneck herb — farm the Hinterlands.

    225–300. Greater Mana Potions (Khadgar's Whisker + Purple Lotus), Elixirs of Greater Agility, and Major Mana Potions at the very top (Dreamfoil country: Felwood, the Plaguelands). Flasks exist at 300 but are raid prep, not a leveling tool.

    Blacksmithing route — what to forge, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    Reality check. The most material-hungry route in the game: expect well over a thousand bars across the whole climb. With Mining feeding you it's a project; buying the metal makes it a fortune. Stones are your early engine because they're cheap.

    1–75. Rough Sharpening Stones, then Rough Grinding Stones and Copper gear pieces as stones turn green.

    75–150. Coarse and Heavy Sharpening/Grinding Stones with Bronze pieces between. Tin supply decides your speed — the Wetlands is your mine.

    150–225. Iron gear and Green Iron pieces early, then Steel and the first Mithril pieces (spurs and bracers are the usual cheap orange crafts) as Mithril flows in from Tanaris.

    225–300. Mithril Spurs and Mithril Coifs first, then Thorium: small pieces like Thorium Bracers — in practice, whatever Thorium craft is orange and cheapest in bars carries this stretch. The Imperial Plate set (learned piece by piece at the top) finishes many smiths' climbs.

    Enchanting route — what to enchant, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    How this one works. You level two ways: disenchanting magic items for materials, and spending those materials on enchants. Pair with Tailoring (make greens, disenchant them) and the route feeds itself. Keep a cheap 'enchanting dummy' item — a pair of bracers you re-enchant forever.

    1–75. Runed Copper Rod first (you need it to enchant at all), then Minor Health enchants on your dummy bracers, disenchanting every green you find.

    75–150. Lesser enchants on bracers and cloaks (health, armor, minor stats). Strange Dust is the fuel — it comes from low-level greens, so disenchant everything.

    150–225. The stat enchants begin (Lesser/Greater stats to chest, stamina to bracers). Vision Dust and Dream Dust carry this bracket — mid-level dungeon greens are your supply.

    225–300. Greater enchants with Illusion Dust and Greater Eternal Essences from level-50+ greens and blues. This is the expensive stretch everyone warned you about: budget for it, or level it slowly off dungeon drops.

    Engineering route — what to build, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    How this one works. Powders, tubes, and parts: engineering levels on its own components. Almost everything you make along the way is also useful (or sellable to other engineers). Keep your crafts — later recipes eat earlier parts.

    1–75. Rough Blasting Powder, Handfuls of Copper Bolts, Copper Tubes. Copper and Rough Stone only — cheap and fast.

    75–150. Coarse Blasting Powder, Bronze Tubes, and your first real gadgets (target dummies, goggles). Bronze = copper + tin, so your Mining bill doubles here.

    150–225. Heavy Blasting Powder, Gold Power Cores, Mithril Tubes and Casings. Iron, then Mithril, is the cost curve — the Badlands and Tanaris fund it.

    225–300. Mithril Gyro-Shot and Casings into Thorium Widgets and Dense Blasting Powder (Thorium + Dense Stone). The last points usually come from Thorium gear pieces and repair bots at the top.

    Leatherworking route — what to sew, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Light Armor Kits and the simplest Light Leather pieces (gloves, bracers). Cured Light Hides from the trainer round out the bracket.

    75–150. Medium Armor Kits, Embossed Leather pieces, and Cured Medium Hides. Your Skinning should be out-earning this easily.

    150–225. Heavy Armor Kits and Heavy Leather pieces — Nightscape gear (a trainer-taught set) is the classic mid-route because it's leather-only, no rare extras.

    225–300. Thick Armor Kits and Wicked Leather pieces, then Rugged Leather gear at the top. The tribal/dragonscale/elemental specializations open at 225 — pick one at 60, not before; the route to 300 is the same either way.

    Tailoring route — what to sew, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Bolts of Linen Cloth (making bolts gives skill at the very start), then Linen bags and simple linen pieces. Humanoid enemies are your 'gathering nodes' — farm quests that target them.

    75–150. Wool: Woolen pieces and the first real bags (Woolen Bags sell at every level). Double-breasted waistcoats and similar wool pieces carry the middle.

    150–225. Silk Cloth from the Scarlet Monastery level range: Silk Headbands and Formal Shirts are the cheap orange staples. Mageweave begins at the top of the bracket.

    225–300. Mageweave bags and gear, then Runecloth: Runecloth Bags and Belts are the standard finish — runecloth drops all over the level-55+ world, and the bags sell forever. Mooncloth (a 4-day cooldown craft at 250+) is money, not a route.

    Cooking route — what to cook, 1–300Tap to open⌄

    1–75. Spiced Bread is trainer-taught and nearly free; Charred Wolf Meat and Roasted Boar fill the rest from your questing kills. Learn recipes from trainers AND from quests — cooking hides recipes in both.

    75–150. Crab Cakes and Cooked Crab Claw (Wetlands crabs), Coyote Steak (Westfall), and whatever your current zone's meat cooks into — check every new town's cooking trainer and innkeeper for recipes; cooking hides them in both places.

    150–225. Soothing Turtle Bisque (a Hillsbrad-side quest recipe), Spider Sausages, and the first fish dishes if you fish. Keep every meat you loot from now on — you'll cook all of it.

    225–300. Artisan Cooking needs the Gadgetzan quest chain (the famous Clamlette Surprise errand) — do it at 225. Then Monster Omelets (giant eggs, Tanaris) and Tender Wolf Steaks (Alterac/Felwood wolves) carry you to 300.

    First Aid route — bandages (and potions), 1–300Tap to open⌄

    The ladder. First Aid is a cloth ladder, in order: Linen Bandages → Heavy Linen → Wool → Heavy Wool → Silk → Heavy Silk → Mageweave → Heavy Mageweave → Runecloth → Heavy Runecloth Bandages. Make the plain version until it turns green, then switch to the Heavy version of the same cloth.

    Forever addition. First Aid also crafts the standard Healing Potions and cures for poison and disease here — worth real skill points in the early brackets and genuinely useful to carry.

    The one quest. At 225 you need the Triage quest for Artisan First Aid (Alliance: Theramore; Horde: Hammerfall in Arathi). It's the famous 'save the soldiers in order' test — heal the most hurt first, and don't let anyone die on your watch.

    Supply tip. Your cloth competes with Tailoring if you have both. If you don't tailor, buy cloth on the Auction House during the week — prices spike on weekends.

    How dungeons work here — read this firstTap to open⌄

    Five players, three jobs. A dungeon group is a party of five: one tank, one healer, three damage dealers. If those words are new, the General Info tab explains them (Groups & dungeons, and the Combat foundations cards — interrupts and aggro are the two skills dungeons test most).

    No dungeon finder. There is no automatic queue in Forever. You form a group yourselves (friends, guild, or the LookingForGroup chat channel), then you all TRAVEL to the dungeon's entrance and walk in. The trip is part of the game — go together, especially through enemy territory.

    Quests are the real reward. Forever deliberately moved dungeon experience INTO the quests: monster XP is the smaller share (a launch hotfix raised it somewhat), quest XP is the big payout. The golden rule: collect ALL of a dungeon's quests, run it once, hand them all in. Running a dungeon with no quests is doing it the slow way.

    Boss loot got better. In Forever, every dungeon boss drops rare (blue) quality gear, and much of it belongs to sets (like the Defias Leather set in the Deadmines) that give bonuses when you wear several pieces. Dungeon gear is a real gearing path now, not a side trip.

    Stay in the level range. A hotfix rule (Nov 13, softened Nov 14): if an enemy is more than 12 levels below any group member, the whole party earns no experience from it. Group with people near your level and run dungeons in their range.

    Blueprints. Dungeon bosses can drop Blueprint recipes for crafting professions — one more reason your group's crafter wants to come along (see the Professions tab).

    Ragefire ChasmTap to open⌄

    Where. Inside Orgrimmar itself — the entrance is in the Cleft of Shadow, in the Horde capital. No travel, no danger getting there (for Horde).

    Levels & size. 13–18, five players, about 30 minutes. The Horde's first dungeon and the gentlest introduction to group play in the game.

    Before you go. Pick up the quests inside Orgrimmar (they're all nearby). Bring food and water; there's nothing else to prepare — this one is the tutorial.

    The route. One winding cave system with no branches that matter: Oggleflint's trogg camp first, then down past the cultists to Taragaman, and Jergosh & Bazzalan share the final chamber. If you're walking downhill, you're going the right way.

    Oggleflint. A big trogg. Tank him facing away from the group; nothing fancy.

    Taragaman the Hungerer. A fire demon. Spread out a little so his fire nova doesn't catch everyone, and burn him down. 🛡️ Tank: pick up the imps he summons the moment they appear. ⚔️ Damage: imps die before boss damage.

    Jergosh the Invoker & Bazzalan. Warlock-type bosses with helper imps and cultists — kill the adds first, interrupt their casts when you can. 💚 Healer: Jergosh's burn over time is the spike — watch whoever he sets alight. ⚔️ Damage: if Conflagration sets YOU burning, run away from the group until it ends.

    Watch out. Lava crawlers patrol; pull them alone. Otherwise this dungeon is forgiving by design.

    Hall of Thanes — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. Beneath Ironforge in Dun Morogh — the Alliance's home dungeon, Forever's mirror to Ragefire Chasm. Horde groups must travel deep into Alliance territory to reach it.

    Levels & size. 13–18, five players. One of the two first dungeons in the game, and the Alliance's starter.

    Before you go. Grab the dungeon quests in Ironforge and Dun Morogh first — in Forever, the quests are where most of a run's experience lives.

    The route. One route through the old dwarven halls beneath the throne room: Anvilmar's Rest first (Faldrim patrols it), the main chamber beyond (Magmatus), the golem's patrol room (Plunder), and the Reliquary of Kings at the very end (Durgen). Linear — the only way to go wrong is letting a patrol or a knockback pull a fresh pack into your fight.

    Faldrim Anvilmar. A thane's ghost patrolling the first hall, Anvilmar's Rest — which is full of Enraged Apparitions, so clear some of them before you pull him. Interrupt his Mind Blast whenever you can. His Curse slows your tank's attack and casting speed by about 20% and lasts for minutes — it lingers after the fight. Annoying, not deadly. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt Mind Blast. 💚 Healer: his curse lingers for minutes after the fight — heal through the next pulls rather than chasing a cure.

    Magmatus. A fire elemental who ALWAYS pulls together with a Dark Iron Summoner — kill the Summoner first. His Fire Nova hits everyone standing near him: the tank drags him away from the group so only the tank eats it. His Combust sets a random player burning over time — healers, watch for it. 🛡️ Tank: drag him away from the group so his Fire Nova only reaches you. ⚔️ Damage: his Dark Iron Summoner dies first. 💚 Healer: Combust burns a random player — spot-heal them.

    Plunder. A stone golem patrolling the room before the final boss. His only real trick is knocking the tank backward — so pull him back into the previous, already-cleared room, or tank with a wall at your back. The knockback is only dangerous if it throws you into a fresh pack. 🛡️ Tank: pull him back into the previous, cleared room, or tank with a wall behind you — the knockback is the whole fight.

    Durgen Dirgehammer (final boss). In the Reliquary of Kings, flanked by two Lesser Stone Golems, with Dark Iron trash around the room's edges. Clear the edges first, kill the two golems, then Durgen. He fears the whole group and bleeds the tank with Rend — golems plus fear plus bleed together are what overwhelm tanks here, which is why the order matters. ⚔️ Damage: the two stone golems die before Durgen. 🛡️ Tank: expect his fear to scatter the group — hold the center of the room.

    Watch out. Horde visitors: the trip in is the dangerous part on a PvP ruleset, not the dungeon.

    Ruins of Lordaeron — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. In the ruined human capital ABOVE the Undercity, in Tirisfal Glades — the Horde's second early dungeon. Alliance groups must cross into Horde territory.

    Levels & size. 15–20, five players. All of its quests open at level 15.

    Before you go. Collect the quests in Tirisfal Glades and the Undercity before entering; stack them all into one run.

    The route. A ruined city, taken in a rough loop: Witherfang patrols King's Alley near the entrance; the Baron waits at the end of the south-east hallway; Viktor's ruined house is south-west (optional); the Abandoned is summoned in Market Square on the west side; Bjork ends the north-west corridor; and Rath'mael stands in the center — you can SEE him from much of the dungeon, but you have to loop around to reach him.

    Roles — the whole run. ⚔️ Damage — in every trash pack, Lordaeron Revivers die FIRST: they resurrect the enemies you have already killed. 💚 Healer — the trash here runs higher-level than the bosses suggest, with heavy shadow damage: this dungeon heals harder than its level implies.

    Witherfang. A spider patrolling King's Alley near the entrance, with broodlings — watch her patrol so she doesn't land on your group mid-trash. Her poison on the tank is the main trick. Kill her first, then her adds. 🛡️ Tank: time the pull between her patrols. ⚔️ Damage: she dies first, her broodlings after.

    The Baron. At the end of the south-east hallway. He cleaves — melee stand BEHIND him — and his knockback also drops the tank's threat, so the tank taunts straight back. Mind where the knockback throws people: into an unpulled pack is the classic accident. (Sources disagree on his full spell list — expect a surprise or two.) 🛡️ Tank: his knockback drops your threat — taunt back instantly. ⚔️ Damage: melee stand BEHIND him (he cleaves).

    Viktor the Vile (optional, secret). In a ruined house to the south-west. Click the Grim Campfire in the fireplace and waves of skeletons come first — Viktor himself, a plain fight, arrives last. The waves are the real cost: your healer will reach Viktor nearly empty, so mana potions and self-heals bridge the gap. 💚 Healer: the skeleton waves will nearly empty you — drink and potion before Viktor himself appears.

    The Abandoned. Summoned at the statue with glowing red eyes in Market Square — walking near it starts three waves of adds, then he comes. His Frost Armor chills anyone who hits him, slowing them. Interrupt or stun his Drain Life on the tank, and expect a Frost Nova that roots everyone near him for a few seconds. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt or stun his Drain Life. 💚 Healer: his Frost Nova roots everyone briefly — heal through the standstill.

    Bjork. At the end of the north-west corridor: a plain tank-and-spank with one knockback. By now you know the rule — don't get knocked into unpulled enemies. 🛡️ Tank: his one trick is a knockback — don't let it throw you into unpulled enemies.

    Rath'mael (final boss). The necromancer in the dungeon's center. Clear everything around him before engaging. Interrupt or stun his Flamestrike, every cast. He radiates a damaging aura that punishes standing near him, so melee take steady damage the whole fight — healers, this one is yours. He drops TWO profession Blueprints: Fermenter (Alchemy) and Arcane Salvager (Enchanting). ⚔️ Damage: interrupt or stun Flamestrike, every cast, no exceptions. 💚 Healer: his aura steadily drains everyone standing in melee — this fight is yours.

    Lordaeron Captain (rare). A rare spawn on the west side — he may only appear after every other boss is dead, and he's FRIENDLY to Alliance, so only Horde groups can fight him. A plain fight; the danger is his long patrol route through trash-dense streets.

    Watch out. Undead casters hurt at this level. Pull one pack at a time and stop their casts (see the interrupts card in General Info). One trash rule above all: Lordaeron Revivers RESURRECT dead enemies — if you see one, it dies first, always, or your cleared packs stand back up.

    Wailing CavernsTap to open⌄

    Where. In the Barrens, in an oasis cave south-west of the Crossroads. The entrance cave itself has enemies — fight your way to the glowing portal.

    Levels & size. 15–24, five players, and LONG — budget 1 to 1.5 hours. A maze of caverns full of druids gone wrong.

    Before you go. It has around 7 quests (mostly Horde, from the Crossroads, Ratchet, and Thunder Bluff; Alliance get fewer). Stack them all — one full clear with every quest is a level or more of experience. Bring water; casters drink a lot here.

    The route. A genuine maze — the trick is that it's really two loops (an east and a west cavern) joined at the middle. The four Fang Lords are spread across both loops; Kresh swims the central river and Skum patrols the upper ledges. Verdan guards the far back. When all four Lords are dead, return to the ENTRANCE for the escort — groups waste the most time here by forgetting where the Disciple waits.

    Roles — the whole run. ⚔️ Damage — throughout: the Fang druids' healing spells get interrupted, in trash packs and boss fights alike. 💚 Healer — keep a mana reserve: at this level the fights spike without much warning.

    The four Lords of the Fang. Lady Anacondra, Lord Cobrahn, Lord Pythas, and Lord Serpentis — corrupted druid bosses spread through the maze. All four must die to unlock the ending. Interrupt their lightning and healing when you can. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt their healing spells before they land. 💚 Healer: Lady Anacondra's Sleep takes a player out of the fight — cover the gap.

    Kresh & Skum. A giant turtle and a thunder lizard — simple tank-and-kill fights on the way.

    Verdan the Everliving. A huge elemental who hits the tank VERY hard for this level. The healer's whole job is this fight; everyone else just burns him. 🛡️ Tank: face him away from the group (his Thunderclap hits everyone in front). 💚 Healer: he hits very hard for this level — this is your big heal check.

    Mutanus the Devourer. The final boss, and he doesn't exist until you finish: after the four Lords die, talk to the Disciple of Naralex at the entrance and escort him to the back — survive the waves, then Mutanus rises. 🛡️ Tank: lead the escort at a pace your healer's mana can sustain. ⚔️ Damage: each wave's casters die first.

    Watch out. It's easy to get lost — stay together and mark your tank. Deviate beasts sleep and fear; pull carefully. The Embrace of the Viper gear set drops here and is worth wearing.

    The DeadminesTap to open⌄

    Where. In Westfall — enter through the barn in Moonbrook and fight through the mine tunnels to the portal. The Alliance's signature first dungeon.

    Levels & size. 17–26, five players, about an hour. A pirate-brotherhood stronghold that ends on a ship.

    Before you go. Do the Westfall Defias quest chain first — it unlocks the dungeon's main quests (about 7 in total, worth a huge chunk of XP in Forever). Kill miners on the way in; they drop a powder used for a door later.

    The route. One direction the whole way: mine tunnels, then three workshop rooms — Rhahk'Zor, Sneed, Gilnid — each behind a door the boss guards. After Gilnid, the foundry opens into Ironclad Cove: Mr. Smite at the ship's gangplank, up the decks past Greenskin to VanCleef at the very top, with Cookie patrolling the ship's lower deck on the way. Look back after each workshop boss: a patrol walks in behind you.

    Rhahk'Zor. An ogre foreman with two helpers — kill the helpers, then him.

    Sneed. A goblin in a giant shredder machine. Destroy the shredder, then Sneed himself hops out — finish him.

    Gilnid & Captain Greenskin. The smelter boss and the ship's first mate — straightforward fights; Greenskin poisons, so the healer watches the tank.

    Mr. Smite. The ship's tauren guard. Twice during the fight he STUNS the whole group and runs to his chest for a bigger weapon. You can't stop it — just survive the stun and keep going. He hits much harder each time. 🛡️ Tank: he stuns the whole group and walks off for a bigger weapon — re-grab him the instant he returns. 💚 Healer: each weapon phase hits harder; the last phase is the healing check. ⚔️ Damage: use the stun pause to bandage and reposition.

    Edwin VanCleef. The final boss on the ship's top deck, leader of the Defias. Near the end he calls two bodyguards — kill them, then finish him. The Defias Leather set drops in here. ⚔️ Damage: burn the two rogues he calls at 50% health — or stun him right as he crosses 50% to stop the call entirely. 🛡️ Tank: pick the adds up if the call lands.

    Miner Johnson (rare). A rare-spawn foreman in a side tunnel before Sneed. He reduces the tank's armor with Pierce Armor, so the tank takes rising damage — a short fight, kill him fast. Check his tunnel; his drops are worth it.

    Captain Greenskin. On the ship's deck before VanCleef, with a pirate crew around him. He cleaves (melee: don't stand in front) and poisons the tank with his harpoon. Kill the squallshaper caster in his crew first.

    Watch out. Patrols walk the tunnels BEHIND you after you pass. Clear as you go and don't run back alone.

    Shadowfang KeepTap to open⌄

    Where. In Silverpine Forest, on a cliff west of the Sepulcher — a haunted castle full of worgen (werewolves).

    Levels & size. 22–30, five players, about 45 minutes. Spooky, compact, and a favorite.

    Before you go. Horde get the main quests from the Undercity/Sepulcher; Alliance have a couple too. Bring someone who can cure curses if you have one — the ghosts love cursing.

    The route. Up through the castle in a spiral: the courtyard and stables, Rethilgore's cell block, Silverlaine's dining hall, then the ramparts — Springvale, Odo, Fenrus and Nandos in some order as you climb — and Arugal alone in the top tower. Almost entirely linear; if a door is locked, the key is on the boss you just passed.

    Commander Springvale. A ghost knight with two guards. Kill the guards first; his holy spells hurt.

    Odo the Blindwatcher & Fenrus. A bat-handler and a huge wolf. Fenrus hits hard and fast — the tank's fight, everyone else burns. ⚔️ Damage: burn Fenrus fast — every second shortens his poison's work.

    Archmage Arugal. The final boss and the man who cursed this place. He teleports around his tower, blasts the group with shadow, and can briefly turn one of you into a worgen. Keep pressure on him wherever he appears and heal through it. 🛡️ Tank: chase his teleports and rebuild threat after every jump. ⚔️ Damage: if he mind-controls a teammate, crowd-control the TEAMMATE — don't kill them.

    Rethilgore & Baron Silverlaine. The early bosses: Rethilgore is a worgen prisoner in the cell block (simple, hard-hitting). Silverlaine, a ghost baron in the dining hall, periodically summons worgen spirits to help him — kill the summons or burn through them. 💚 Healer: Rethilgore's Soul Drain channels straight through the tank — heal through it. ⚔️ Damage: Silverlaine's summoned worgen die when they appear.

    Wolf Master Nandos. On the ramparts with a pack of wolves. Kill the wolves first — Nandos himself is a plain fight once they're gone. 🛡️ Tank: hold him and his wolves together, so nothing reaches the healer.

    Watch out. The courtyard and stable packs link together — pull back into cleared space instead of charging in.

    Blackfathom DeepsTap to open⌄

    Where. On the coast of Ashenvale — you swim down through a flooded temple entrance to reach the portal. Yes, you swim; don't panic.

    Levels & size. 24–32, five players, about an hour. An underwater temple of naga and Twilight cultists.

    Before you go. Quests from Ashenvale and (for Horde) the Stonetalon foothills. Light the braziers as you go — the dungeon's torch puzzle opens the way forward.

    The route. Swim down into the temple, then work through flooded halls in one line: Ghamoo-ra's pool, the shrine rooms (light the braziers to open the way), Lady Sarevess's chamber, Old Serra'kis in the water, Kelris's sanctuary, and Aku'mai's pool at the very bottom. The brazier room is the only puzzle — light all four torches.

    Ghamoo-ra. A giant turtle in a pool. Simple, but his shell makes him slow to kill — patience.

    Twilight Lord Kelris. The dangerous one: he puts the tank (or whoever he chooses) to SLEEP mid-fight. Whoever isn't asleep keeps fighting; the sleeper wakes after a few seconds. Kill his cultist adds first.

    Aku'mai. A three-headed hydra in the final pool — the tank holds it at the pool's edge and everyone burns it down. 🛡️ Tank: face his poison spray away from the group. ⚔️ Damage: switch to his summoned elementals when they appear.

    Lady Sarevess & Old Serra'kis. Sarevess is a naga priestess in a side chamber — she freezes a player in ice; break them out by killing her fast and keeping the tank up. Serra'kis is a giant threshadon in the water: a simple, slow tank fight — don't fall in after him. ⚔️ Damage: spread out — Forked Lightning chains between grouped players. 💚 Healer: keep everyone topped so a chain can't finish anyone.

    Baron Aquanis. An optional water elemental summoned at the shrine after Kelris (requires the quest item). Groups on a timer skip him; completionists get a solid caster drop.

    Watch out. Murlocs. They flee at low health and bring friends. Kill runners fast, or crowd-control them.

    The StockadeTap to open⌄

    Where. Inside Stormwind itself — a prison in the Canals district. Alliance home dungeon; Horde would have to invade the capital, so it's Alliance-only in practice.

    Levels & size. 24–32, five players, and SHORT — about 30 minutes. Three wings of rioting prisoners.

    Before you go. The quests come from Stormwind and the Redridge/Westfall chains (the riot's backstory). This dungeon's loot is thin — you run it for the quest XP, which in Forever is the point.

    The route. Three short wings off one central hall — clear them in any order: Kam Deepfury's wing, Targorr's wing, Dextren Ward's wing, with Hamhock at the back of one. Thirty minutes, no map needed, just watch the runners.

    Kam Deepfury, Targorr the Dread, Dextren Ward. The three wing bosses — all simple tank-and-kill fights. Hamhock, an ogre at the back of one wing, is the only one who hits notably hard. 🛡️ Tank: Dextren's Fear can drag you into unopened cells — tank with your back to a cleared wall. 💚 Healer: the fear fights scatter damage across the whole group; watch everyone. ⚔️ Damage: burst Hamhock down during his Bloodlust, before he bursts the tank.

    Bruegal Ironknuckle (rare). A rare-spawn dwarf prisoner who sometimes appears in a side cell — the hardest hitter in the prison. If he's up, treat him like a fourth boss.

    Watch out. Prisoners run at low health and pull the next cell. Fight in doorways and finish runners.

    Excavation Site — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. In the Wetlands, at the excavation near Thelgen Rock and Dun Algaz — a dwarven dig that dug up something it shouldn't have.

    Levels & size. 24–29, five players. One of Forever's new dungeons, sitting between Blackfathom Deeps and Scarlet Monastery in the leveling path.

    Before you go. Grab the Wetlands quests for the dig site first. As with every Forever dungeon: quests first, then run it once, fully.

    The route. An open-air dig worked in one direction: across the lake shallows to Saltspine, up through the crocodile den and the bushes (clear them properly) to Shadetooth, then into the relic chamber where the Relic Guardian waits, switched off, beside its control panel. Three bosses, no branches — hard to get lost.

    Saltspine. First boss: a huge crocolisk in the lake shallows. His Dust Storm drops melee hit chance by 25% — ranged players barely notice, melee just push through. He also tears the tank's tendons, slowing them down. A simple opener. ⚔️ Damage: ranged barely notice his Dust Storm — shoot freely. Melee: push through the misses, your job doesn't change.

    Shadetooth. The hard one — a raptor boss by the crocodile den, with stealthed raptors hiding in the bushes around him: clear them first. He pulls with two Thicket Hunters whose Infected Wound cuts the healing your tank receives (cure it; if you have no crowd control, kill the Hunters first). Shadetooth himself fears the whole group, rends the tank hard, and ENRAGES at 30% health — save your stuns for that. 💚 Healer: dispel the Hunters' Infected Wound on sight — it cuts the healing its target receives, and it's this fight's hidden killer. ⚔️ Damage: his two Hunters die first, and do NOT crowd-control them — they must die, fast. Save stuns for his enrage at 30%. 🛡️ Tank: his Rend stacks its bleed on you — call for the burn at 30%.

    Relic Guardian. The final boss: a stone guardian standing switched-off until someone clicks the Archival Panel beside him. He constantly knocks back and stuns the tank, and his Thunderclap slows everyone in melee range hard — stay out of melee if you don't need to be there. Despite being a machine, most crowd control works on him. 🛡️ Tank: constant knockbacks and stuns — tank with the chamber wall behind you.

    Watch out. New dungeon etiquette: pull slowly until your group learns the packs. Nobody will judge you — nobody knows it yet either.

    Scarlet MonasteryTap to open⌄

    Where. In Tirisfal Glades, north-east of the Undercity — a monastery of fanatical Scarlet Crusade humans. It has FOUR separate wings, each its own dungeon.

    Levels & size. 26–45 across the wings: Graveyard 26–36, Library 29–39, Armory 32–42, Cathedral 35–45. Five players, 30–45 minutes per wing. You'll return here across many levels.

    Before you go. Horde get the key quests from the Undercity; the Library drops the Scarlet Key that opens the Armory and Cathedral doors. Do the wings in order: Graveyard → Library → Armory → Cathedral.

    The route. Four separate entrances in one courtyard — you zone into each wing separately. Graveyard: one crypt loop ending at Thalnos. Library: a long hall (Loksey's hounds early) to Doan. Armory: a courtyard and training rooms to Herod. Cathedral: one great hall — clear ALL of it — then the altar where Mograine and Whitemane wait.

    Bloodmage Thalnos (Graveyard). An undead caster who raises skeletons and rains fire. Kill the raised adds, interrupt his fire when you can.

    Arcanist Doan (Library). A mage who, at half health, casts a huge Arcane Explosion — when he starts casting it, EVERYONE runs away from him, then runs back in. 💚 Healer: his Arcane Explosion is the big group hit — pre-top everyone. ⚔️ Damage: spread out.

    Herod (Armory). The famous one. He periodically spins in a whirlwind ('Blades of Light') — melee step OUT while he spins, step back in when he stops. At the end he enrages; burn him. 🛡️ Tank & ⚔️ Damage: when his Whirlwind starts, EVERYONE steps out — nobody chases him through it.

    Mograine & Whitemane (Cathedral). Kill Commander Mograine first. When he falls, High Inquisitor Whitemane sleeps your whole group and RESURRECTS him — wake up, kill Mograine again, then kill her. She also heals, so interrupt her when possible. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt Whitemane's heal, and stop Mograine's resurrection at all costs. 🛡️ Tank: grab Whitemane the moment she joins the fight. 💚 Healer: she sleeps the whole group first — call for the interrupt if yours is down.

    Interrogator Vishas (Graveyard). In the torture chamber off the crypt: he interrogates — and damages — one player at a time. Simple fight; the room also holds the torturer's tools your quests may want.

    Houndmaster Loksey (Library). Early in the Library with four bloodhounds. The hounds are the fight: kill them first while the tank holds Loksey, or they shred your cloth wearers.

    Watch out. Scarlet runners flee and bring whole rooms. In the Cathedral, clear the entire hall before pulling the bosses, or the whole room joins the fight.

    City of Dalaran — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. The floating city's ground level in the Alterac Mountains — you enter through the SEWERS. Getting in is a quest, not a walk-up: this dungeon has an attunement.

    Levels & size. 28–33, five players. The magic city of Dalaran, from underneath.

    Before you go. ATTUNEMENT REQUIRED: the Dalaran Sewer Key, from a quest chain in Hillsbrad Foothills (Alliance side starts around Southshore, Horde around Tarren Mill). Alliance players can also reach the sewer entrance through the city itself; for Horde the key is effectively mandatory, since the city above is hostile to them. Do not travel there without sorting entry first. Then stack the dungeon's quests as usual.

    The route. Two halves. The sewers come first, in fixed order: Atrexis mid-sewers, the secret boss Lyn at the far end of the docks (optional), then up to Krasus' Landing. The surface city is free-flowing: the Arcane Anomaly at the landing, the Mana Devourer at the Violet Hold, the Fel Ancient in the central square, the Unstable Sentinel at the north end by the profession buildings, the Arcanic Enigma north-east by the Antonidas statue — and the Shade of the Archmage inside the Violet Citadel, reached by talking to Archmage Modera at its base.

    Atrexis the Grave Knight. Mid-sewers, patrolling near Kirin Tor Necromancers — clear a couple of them before you pull him. He disarms the tank and continually raises non-elite skeletons, and he buffs himself and his allies with Unholy Frenzy (dispel it if your group can). Kill the first few skeletons, then burn him down. 🛡️ Tank: he disarms you and keeps raising skeletons — hold the skeletons' attention so they never reach the healer. ⚔️ Damage: kill the first few skeletons, then burn him; dispel his Unholy Frenzy if your class can.

    Lyn the Ignored (secret, optional). Summoned at the purple runes at the far end of the sewer docks — but only with the Tome of Dalaran: loot the Black Tome from the table on Arugal's platform in Shadowfang Keep, take it to the Ambermill Leyline Focus in Silverpine Forest to transform it, then use it here. Her Shadow Bolt Volley hits the whole group and cannot be interrupted — heal through it. Her Curse of Thorns makes your tank take damage every time they attack: decurse it if you can. She raises skeletons, too. 💚 Healer: her Shadow Bolt Volley cannot be interrupted — this fight is pure healing throughput. ⚔️ Damage: decurse the tank's Curse of Thorns if your class can (he hurts himself by attacking).

    Arcane Anomaly. At Krasus' Landing, up on the surface. A rotating arcane beam sweeps the room counter-clockwise — touching it can kill you. Stay near the center and keep rotating with the room (the center is safer anyway: adds spawn at the room's edges). Interrupt the Arcane Bolts pelting your tank. ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: stay near the center and rotate with the beam — never cross it. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt the Arcane Bolts pelting the tank.

    Mana Devourer. At the Violet Hold — and ALL the trash in its area joins the fight when he's pulled, so clear the whole area first. Every ~50 seconds his Debilitate damages and drains about 200 mana from the ENTIRE group: this fight is a race. Kill him before your healer runs dry. He also leaves a damage-over-time effect on the tank. 💚 Healer: he drains about 200 mana from the whole group every ~50 seconds — budget your mana from the first second. ⚔️ Damage: this fight is a race: kill him before your healer runs dry.

    Fel Ancient. In the central square. He roots two players in place (Entangle, with nature damage ticking) and drops poison pools under targeted players (Fel Timbers). The killer is the overlap — rooted inside a pool dies fast. Save root-breaking tools like Blessing of Freedom for exactly that moment, and move out of pools instantly. ⚔️ Damage: rooted inside his poison pool is the killer combo — save root-breakers like Blessing of Freedom for exactly that overlap, and move out of pools instantly.

    Unstable Sentinel. At the north end, by the profession buildings. Before pulling, kill the Angry Tomes around him — left alive, they spawn from the buildings during the fight. His Malfunction is a huge blast centered on himself (about 25 yards): ranged players and healers can out-range it completely by standing at maximum range; melee run out every time. Drops Blueprint: Spinning Wheel (Tailoring). ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: stand at maximum range — his Malfunction blast can't reach you there. ⚔️ Damage: kill the Angry Tomes around him BEFORE the pull; left alive, they join mid-fight.

    Arcanic Enigma. North-east, by the Antonidas statue, flanked by two Mana Phantoms that Mana Burn your casters — kill the Phantoms first. He periodically silences everyone near him (casters: stay at maximum range), summons small manaling adds for the tank to scoop up, and casts Manamorph — dispel it, whoever in your group can remove magic effects. ⚔️ Damage: his two Mana Phantoms die first (they Mana Burn your casters). Dispel his Manamorph, whoever in the group can. 💚 Healer: stay at maximum range — he silences everyone near him.

    Shade of the Archmage (final boss). Inside the Violet Citadel: talk to Archmage Modera at the bottom level and she opens the portal to him. He spams Arcane Bolts at the tank and casts Arcane Explosion — ranged and healers stay at maximum range. Spread out so his Bounding Mana projectiles can't splash between you. The twist: his spells cost him mana, and if he ever runs dry he Mass-Polymorphs your whole group and Evocates back to full — so don't try to drain him; it only resets the fight. Just kill him. Drops Blueprint: Arcane Salvager (Enchanting). ⚔️ Damage: do NOT drain his mana — emptying it makes him polymorph the whole group and Evocate back to full, resetting the fight. Spread out for his Bounding Mana. 💚 Healer: maximum range keeps you out of his Arcane Explosion.

    Watch out. Because of the attunement, groups form more slowly for this one — plan it with your regular group rather than hoping for a pickup. Also: some bosses here shrug off Arcane damage — Arcane Mages, bring your other schools.

    Where. In Dun Morogh, west of Ironforge — the fallen gnome capital, a vast irradiated techno-city. Horde can teleport in: a quest chain from the goblin engineers in Orgrimmar sends you to Booty Bay for a transponder.

Through the thorn maze in a rough line: the entrance pits (Agathelos below), up through the crone's followers — Ramtusk's war band and Jargba's chamber on the way — to Charlga at the top, with the goblin escort event in the last stretch. Jargba, in a chamber nearby, is a caster who raises the dead and fears; interrupt him and kill his summons. Both are simple once their helpers are dead. ⚔️ Damage: Ramtusk's totems die on sight. 💚 Healer: Jargba mind-controls a player — watch the controlled player's health in both directions.

    The Drowned City — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. Off the coast of Stranglethorn Vale, west of Grom'gol — the entrance is UNDERWATER. You swim down to a sunken city.

    Levels & size. 35–40, five players. One of Forever's new dungeons and the first underwater one — it was the showpiece dungeon at BlizzCon.

    Before you go. Water breathing matters here: a Warlock's Unending Breath, Shaman's Water Breathing, or Elixirs of Water Breathing from an Alchemist all help if any part requires long swims. Ask before you go. Boss facts below are cross-checked from community clear guides (wow.gg and others) — Icy Veins covers this dungeon's quests but hasn't published a boss guide for it.

    The route. In through the flooded entrance and up the hill to Zul'Alai, then the naga quarter: Var'Taka — and click the glowing PEARL after him to drop the barrier forward. Around the central pool to Captain Dreadrise (a second pearl after him), west to Deathless Marrow's altar, and Min'loth at the far west end. The flooded Deep Archives, with an optional boss, branch off the room near Var'Taka. Come at 37+ — the listed range is generous.

    Zul'Alai. An undead troll at the top of the entry hill. He hits the tank hard and slams everyone standing close (it stuns) — ranged and the healer stay out of melee. Around 30% health he enrages: save tank defensives and your burst for the last stretch. 🛡️ Tank: his slam stuns everyone in melee range — you should be the only one standing in it. 💚 Healer: save the big heals for his enrage under 30%.

    Var'Taka. A naga fought with two Brinescale Priestesses who HEAL and throw Frostbolts — kill them or crowd-control them first. Var'Taka cleaves in front (tank faces him away) and his Harpoon yanks a random player to him; if it's you, just walk back. Click the glowing pearl after the fight to open the way. ⚔️ Damage: his two priestesses die first, or stay crowd-controlled — they heal him. 🛡️ Tank: face his cleave away from the group; whoever his Harpoon yanks in just walks back.

    Captain Dreadrise. An orc pirate captain by the central pool. His Embrine traps a random player in a water bubble where they DROWN — every damage dealer switches to the bubble and breaks it, immediately, every time. Interrupt his Waterbolt Volley. Clear the arena first so a patrol doesn't join mid-fight. Second pearl after him. ⚔️ Damage: his water bubble is a drowning timer — EVERYONE switches to it, instantly, every time. One assigned interrupt owns his Waterbolt Volley.

    Deathless Marrow. An elemental at a western altar — and widely called the hardest fight in the dungeon (it was toned down in a Nov 9 hotfix). He endlessly summons undead adds while his Bloodbolt hits the whole group. The tank gathers the adds, damage cleaves them down near the boss — but keep real damage on Marrow himself: killing only the adds never ends the fight, it just empties your healer. 🛡️ Tank: gather his endless summoned adds beside him so they can be cleaved down. ⚔️ Damage: cleave the adds, but keep your real damage on Marrow — killing only adds never ends this fight.

    Min'loth the Serpent (final boss). Two fights in one. Phase 1, troll form: Drench on the tank, Deluge damage on the group. At zero health he transforms into a giant serpent at FULL health — phase 2 adds a group-wide Fear and a Poison Bolt Volley. Clear his room completely first (fear plus a spare pack is the classic wipe here), save your resources for phase 2, and cleanse the poison fast. 💚 Healer: phase 2 poisons the whole group — cleanse promptly. ⚔️ Damage: save cooldowns for the serpent phase, and the room must be fully cleared first: his fear will find any leftover pack.

    Primeval Elemental (optional). Hidden in the flooded Deep Archives: kill every Undying Offering in the flooded room near Var'Taka and it appears. The fight is underwater — watch your breath meter, interrupt its Waterbolt, and spread out for Freeze Water (group damage plus a slow). Drops a strong caster trinket.

    Watch out. Underwater movement is slower and 3D — enemies can come from above and below. Stay close together; it's easy to separate. The main wipe cause here is body-pulling extra packs near the fear effects — pull back, always.

    Razorfen DownsTap to open⌄

    Where. Also in the southern Barrens, east of Razorfen Kraul — a quilboar burial ground taken over by the undead Scourge.

    Levels & size. 37–46, five players, about an hour. Undead everywhere: priests and paladins shine (Turn Undead, Exorcism).

    Before you go. Quests from the Barrens and Thousand Needles. Someone should bring the ability to cure diseases — the ghouls spread them.

    The route. In the door, past the idol room (Mordresh), up onto the burial mound for Tuten'kash's gong, then the long spiral: Glutton patrols the path up, and Amnennar waits at the very top with his skeletal court.

    Tuten'kash. A giant spider behind a gong. Hitting the gong starts waves of spiders before she comes herself — let the tank hit the gong, group up, and burn the waves together. ⚔️ Damage: let the tank ring the gongs between waves — rushing is how everything arrives at once.

    Amnennar the Coldbringer. The final boss, a lich on the burial mound. He summons skeletons and chills the group — kill the summons, keep the healer's mana for him. 💚 Healer: his frost aura is constant group damage for the whole fight. ⚔️ Damage: kill his summoned specters when they appear.

    Mordresh Fire Eye & Glutton. Mordresh is a skeleton caster by the great idol who surrounds himself with fire — kill his skeleton escorts first and interrupt his casts. Glutton is an abomination patrolling the spiral path: pure tank damage, nothing clever — just a lot of health. ⚔️ Damage: run OUT of Mordresh's Fire Nova — it only hits close range. 💚 Healer: cure Glutton's diseases if your class can.

    Watch out. The spiral mound at the end is one long linked fight if you rush it. Pull it in slices.

    Krol'dok Stronghold — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. In Riverglades — a stronghold dungeon from Forever's new set, bridging the gap between Razorfen Downs and Uldaman.

    Levels & size. 40–45, five players.

    Before you go. Collect its quests in the zone first, as always. At this level, also start saving: your mount money should be nearly done, and dungeon blues replace quest greens fast here. Boss facts below are cross-checked from community clear guides (Method and others).

    The route. One road through a slave-mine stronghold, boss after boss: Grox patrols the mines at the start, Zarmok's cultist compound sits up the road, Ashka waits by the forge, Nar'gurk stands with his warlocks, then the bridge — an event crossing — to Bossman Toof, and Overlord Mugg's house at the road's end.

    Roles — the whole run. ⚔️ Damage — Twilight Peons are runners: stun or slow them before they alert the next pack. Krol'dok Shaman Bloodlust their allies — they die, or eat an interrupt, before their warriors get buffed.

    Slavecrusher Grox. An ogre patrolling the mines at the start. His Force Compliance drags nearby prisoners into the fight (he'll grab at least one even from a cleared area) and he Bloodlusts himself and his allies. Clear a fighting spot first, burn the called prisoners fast, and the healer pre-covers the tank through his 2-second Knockdown stuns. He drops a profession Blueprint (Expert Campfire, for Cooking) — your group's cook wants this kill. ⚔️ Damage: burn the prisoners he calls into the fight. 💚 Healer: his Knockdown stuns the tank for 2 seconds at a time — pre-cover those windows.

    Twilight Commander Zarmok. A cultist commander patrolling among grunt packs — clear the packs and time your pull between his patrols. Heavy Strike on the tank, a Rending Whirl that hits everyone in melee, and an Enrage at 30%. Melee-light groups have a noticeably easier time here. 🛡️ Tank: pull him between patrols, with his grunt packs already cleared. ⚔️ Damage: melee step OUT of his Rending Whirl; his enrage at 30% is the burn phase.

    War Handler Ashka. By the forge, with two wolf pets — Snarlfang and Terrorhowl. Her Command: Kill! sends the wolves to fixate a random player and maul them. Kill the pets first; if you have a Paladin, Blessing of Protection on the fixated player sends the wolves back to the tank. ⚔️ Damage: her two wolves die first. If the wolves fixate you, a Paladin's Blessing of Protection sends them back to the tank.

    Nar'gurk. An ogre mage standing with a curseweaver and two warlocks — crowd-control or interrupt the casters as you pull. His Convert mind-controls one player until the group beats them down to 50% health: free them with careful, gentle damage, not your biggest hits. His Shadow Bolt Volley hits everyone. Mercy rule: his adds do NOT respawn if you wipe, so attempts get easier. ⚔️ Damage: crowd-control his caster companions, and free his mind-controlled player with GENTLE damage — they need to reach 50% health, not 0%. 💚 Healer: watch the controlled player's health in both directions.

    Bossman Toof (the bridge). Crossing the bridge is the fight: engaging the two Centurions at its start makes pairs of Brutes keep spawning as you cross. Toof waits at the far end with two Shaman. His Mortal Strike halves the healing your tank receives — kill the Shaman first (they can Bloodlust him), then Toof; if a fresh Brute wave lands mid-fight, control it rather than chasing it. ⚔️ Damage: his two Shaman die first — they can Bloodlust him. 💚 Healer: his Mortal Strike HALVES the healing your tank receives: pre-heal before strikes land.

    Overlord Mugg (final boss). In the house at the road's end, guarded by two Centurions — kill or control them first. Mugg is a weapon-swap fight (Mr. Smite's bigger cousin): he starts on Maces, later swaps to Mauls that hit far harder, and his Fury speeds his attacks up with every hit he lands until the next swap — tank avoidance stats quietly shine here. His Crushing Slam leaps onto a ranged player: spread out. He drops the Krol'dok Ogre Key, a shortcut for future runs. 🛡️ Tank: his Fury speeds him up with every hit he lands — avoidance and your cooldowns belong in the Mauls phase. ⚔️ Damage: spread out — his Crushing Slam leaps onto a ranged player.

    Watch out. Organized enemies punish the 'charge in' habit — let the tank pull every pack back to safe ground. Specifics: Twilight Grunts whirlwind (melee step OUT), Twilight Peons are runners who sprint to alert other packs (stun or slow them), and Krol'dok Shaman Bloodlust their allies — kill or interrupt them before their warrior friends get buffed.

    Where. In the Badlands, in an ancient titan vault the dwarves are excavating. There's a back entrance in the north of the zone once you know it.

    Levels & size. 41–51, five players, about 1–1.5 hours. The last bosses are much higher level than the first — groups often do it in two visits.

    Before you go. A long quest web (Badlands, Ironforge/Orgrimmar, and a chain that starts inside). Two items combine into the staff that opens the final chamber — the group needs the pieces from mid-bosses, so don't leave early.

The classic 'adds in phases' fight. 🛡️ Tank: pick up each stone statue the moment it wakes. ⚔️ Damage: switch to awakened statues as they come — they out-threat the boss. 💚 Healer: a long fight: mana discipline early pays off late.

    Watch out. The trogg and golem packs hit hard for their level. And keep the staff pieces: leaving without them means no final boss next time either.

    Zul'FarrakTap to open⌄

    Where. In Tanaris, in the desert — a sand troll city, mostly open-air. Bring sunscreen jokes; everyone makes them.

    Levels & size. 44–54, five players, about an hour. A beloved dungeon with one of the game's most famous fights.

    Before you go. Quests from Gadgetzan and Steamwheedle Port. One boss (Gahz'rilla) needs a special mallet from a quest chain that starts in the Hinterlands — if your group has it, great; if not, skip that boss.

An open-air city — mostly one sweep: in the gates, past Theka's tomb and Antu'sul's pool, up the great pyramid for the execution event, then Zum'rah's graveyard corner, Velratha and Gahz'rilla (gong, needs the mallet) by the pools, and Ukorz & Ruuzlu in the final temple. If you can see the pyramid, you can navigate.

    The pyramid event. Halfway in, you free prisoners at the top of a huge stepped pyramid — then waves of trolls charge UP the steps at you while an executioner boss waits at the bottom. Stay at the top, let them come to you, drink between waves. This fight teaches 'let them come to us' better than anything. 💚 Healer: a marathon of waves — conserve mana from the very first step. ⚔️ Damage: clear each wave fully before stepping down; never pull the whole staircase.

    Chief Ukorz Sandscalp. The final boss, fought with his witch doctor partner Ruuzlu — kill Ruuzlu first (he heals), then the Chief. 🛡️ Tank: he and Ruuzlu fight as a pair — hold both, faced away from the group.

    Antu'sul & Witch Doctor Zum'rah. Antu'sul guards the sacred pool with basilisk pets — kill the basilisks (their gaze can stun) or control them. Zum'rah, in the graveyard corner, raises zombie trolls and drops a healing ward: kill the WARD first, every time, then him. ⚔️ Damage: totems die on sight, and Zum'rah's raised zombies before the boss. 💚 Healer: Antu'sul's earthquake damage is group-wide.

    Gahz'rilla. The giant hydra in the pool, summoned by banging the gong — but only with the Mallet of Zul'Farrak from the Hinterlands quest chain. She breathes frost over the group; spread out, keep the tank topped, and collect one of the era's famous swords if luck smiles. 🛡️ Tank: he knocks back — wall behind you. ⚔️ Damage: spread out, and kill him fast.

    Watch out. Sandfury witch doctors turn players into frogs and drop healing totems — kill their totems on sight. Totems are always priority one in troll dungeons.

    MaraudonTap to open⌄

    Where. In Desolace, in a sacred centaur burial cave. It has two entrances (the 'purple' and 'orange' sides) that meet in the middle, plus a waterfall shortcut.

    Levels & size. 46–55, five players, and huge — most groups do one side per visit (about an hour each).

    Before you go. The quest chain inside leads to the Scepter of Celebras, which opens a portal shortcut straight to the inner half. Worth doing once. Bring nature-resistance if you have any — the inner half is poison-heavy.

Both sides meet at Celebras — kill him for the scepter quest — then the inner sanctum: Rotgrip in the lake below and Princess Theradras at the bottom of the waterfall gorge. With the Scepter of Celebras you can portal straight to the inner half.

    Princess Theradras. The final boss, an earth princess at the bottom. She fears the group and knocks everyone back — tank her with your back to a wall, and don't stand near the edge of her platform. 🛡️ Tank: she fears AND knocks back — wall behind you, always. 💚 Healer: her dust cloud is steady group damage; top up between casts.

    The other bosses. Noxxion (a poison elemental — cure poison or heal hard), Razorlash (a plant giant, plain), Lord Vyletongue (a satyr who summons imps — kill them), Tinkerer Gizlock (a goblin with grenades, plain), Landslide (a rock giant who summons small rock adds), and Rotgrip, a huge crocolisk in the inner lake — an honest tank-and-burn. None has a trick beyond its line here. ⚔️ Damage: Noxxion's spawned copies and Vyletongue's imps die before their bosses. 🛡️ Tank: gather the stone shards Landslide summons.

    Watch out. The inner caverns' poison elementals and plants stack nasty damage-over-time effects. Cure poison if your class can.

    Alcaz Prison — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. On Alcaz Island, off the coast of Dustwallow Marsh — a prison island. Getting there is a swim or a careful boat-side run past high-level enemies.

    Levels & size. 48–53, five players. One of Forever's new dungeons, filling the gap before the level-60 set.

    Before you go. The island around the entrance is dangerous for its level — travel as a group, not solo. Grab the Dustwallow/Theramore-side quests first. Boss facts below come from community clear guides (Mobalytics and others); Alcaz tactics are the newest and least settled on this page.

    The route. Two floors. Upper floor, cell corridors like the Stockade: Ca'zass straight ahead at the main corridor's end, and Wrex patrolling the left corridor's round room. Wrex's keyring opens Maximum Security below: Katerina down the right-hand path, then Blazeroar in the largest chamber. The Questioner — upstairs, in the room at the first crossing — is deliberately fought LAST, after the final boss, for a quest reason explained on his line below.

    Tidemistress Ca'zass. A naga sea witch at the end of the main corridor, with two Strashaz Enchantresses. All three cast Frostbolt Volley — the fight's big damage — and Polymorph. Interrupt every volley, crowd-control one enchantress, and kill the adds first. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt the Frostbolt Volleys — all three casters, every cast. 💚 Healer: pre-top the group; a slipped volley is the damage spike.

    Overseer Wrex. He patrols a round room off the left corridor, with three side rooms around it. The trap: pulling him aggros EVERY living enemy in all three side rooms at once. Wait for him to patrol away, clear each side room one at a time, and then he's easy. He drops the keyring that opens Maximum Security below. 🛡️ Tank: never pull him while any of his three side rooms still lives — pulling him brings all of them.

    Katerina the Cleaner. Downstairs, with two Alcaz Chaplain adds who heal — kill them first, then her. Her own trick is teleporting behind a ranged player to backstab them; it looks alarming and does little damage. (The chained orc behind her is a Horde quest NPC — don't hit him.) ⚔️ Damage: her two healing chaplains die first, then her. 💚 Healer: her teleport-backstabs look alarming and hit softly — don't panic-heal them.

From then on: the tank faces her AWAY from the group, and everyone else stands behind her. 🛡️ Tank: in her dragon phase (from ~20%), face her away from the group, permanently. ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: everyone else stands behind her from that point on.

Back upstairs, in the room at the first crossing — deliberately saved for after Blazeroar, because he drops the Ornate Shackle Key for a quest you only pick up inside the dungeon once the final boss is dead. The confusing one, simplified: clear the entrance level, do the basement statue puzzle (six statues, set order), then the upper balconies in a circle — the six Atal'ai troll bosses and Ogom — before the central pit opens: Atal'alarion below, then Jammal'an's chamber, the dragonkin Hazzas & Morphaz, and finally the Shade of Eranikus in the deepest room.     Where. Inside Blackrock Mountain (between the Burning Steppes and Searing Gorge) — a whole Dark Iron dwarf CITY underground. The biggest dungeon in the game.

    Levels & size. 52–60, five players, and enormous: a full clear takes 3+ hours. Nobody does it in one sitting — it's really two dungeons: the Detention Block (lower) and the Upper City (emperor).

    Before you go. Get the Shadowforge Key: its quest chain starts from a ghost you can only talk to while DEAD near the mountain's central chamber (die once on purpose nearby — really). The key opens shortcuts that make repeat visits sane. There are 15+ quests; stack them across multiple visits.

    The route. A city, not a corridor — pick a destination per visit. Detention Block loop: Gerstahn, Roccor, Grebmar, the arena (watch or fight), Bael'Gar. Upper City: Angerforge, Argelmach and the golem vault, the Grim Guzzler bar, Flamelash, the Tomb of the Seven, the Lyceum, Magmus, and the Emperor's throne room. The Shadowforge Key turns locked doors into a sensible loop; without it, expect backtracking.

    Roles — the whole run. 🛡️ Tank — this is a city, not a corridor: choose one destination per visit and pull ONE room at a time. 💚 Healer — the arena event and the Lyceum are mana marathons: drink at every pause.

    Lord Incendius. A fire elemental on a bridge of lava — the tank keeps him out of the lava; everyone else stays out of his fire nova range or heals through it.

    The Lyceum. A room of endless fast-respawning dwarves where you must light two braziers with torches dropped by special enemies. Constant motion, no stopping — the game's first 'keep moving' room. 🛡️ Tank & ⚔️ Damage: keep moving — lingering is what kills groups here.

    Emperor Dagran Thaurissan. The final boss, in the throne room, with Princess Moira beside him. If you have the quest to save her, DON'T kill her — keep her alive (she heals him, annoyingly) and burn the Emperor; if not, kill her first. Know which run you're doing BEFORE the pull. 💚 Healer: if your group is sparing Moira for the quest, you are healing through her casts too — budget your mana for it.

    Also in the city. High Interrogator Gerstahn (the Detention Block's warden — a shadow priest, interrupt her), Lord Roccor (a fire giant on the lower ring), Houndmaster Grebmar (his dogs are the fight), Bael'Gar (a huge fire giant — spread for his nova), General Angerforge (calls soldier waves at health marks), Golem Lord Argelmach (two golem guards — kill them with him, not after), Ambassador Flamelash (summons fire elementals — burn him before they pile up), Fineous Darkvire (the architect who drops the Shadowforge Key's other half of your sanity), and the Tomb of the Seven: seven ghost dwarves, one at a time, each with a different school of magic — interrupt everything. ⚔️ Damage: in the Tomb of the Seven, the caster dwarves get interrupted before anything else. Golem Lord Argelmach's golems die when he summons them.

    Watch out. The Grim Guzzler bar halfway through can be crossed peacefully (buy the barman a drink / do a small quest) or as a giant bar fight. Choose deliberately — the fight is fun, the shortcut is faster.

    Dire MaulTap to open⌄

    Where. In Feralas — a ruined night elf city with THREE separate wings: East, West, and North, each its own dungeon with its own entrance.

    Levels & size. 55–60, five players, about an hour per wing. Released as the 'catch-up' dungeon set: its gear is excellent for fresh 60s.

    Before you go. East is the easiest start. West needs the Crescent Key (from East's imp-chase boss, Pusillin — chase him through the dungeon as he runs). North has the famous alternative: the TRIBUTE run.

    The route. Three wings, three entrances around one courtyard. East: chase Pusillin through the garden (he runs — four stops), Hydrospawn's pools, Lethtendris, then Alzzin's courtyard. West: the library and pylon halls — Tendris, then de-power the pylons to reach Immol'thar and Tortheldrin. North: the ogre gauntlet — Mol'dar, Kreeg, Fengus, Slip'kik, Kromcrush — ending at King Gordok's throne.

    Alzzin the Wildshaper (East). A satyr who shapeshifts and summons plant adds at the end of a garden courtyard. Kill the adds as they sprout.

    Prince Tortheldrin (West). The final boss of West, guarded by a force field: you must deactivate the pylons around the library (each guarded) to drop his shield. His demon advisor Immol'thar comes first — an eye-beast that beams the group. 🛡️ Tank: scoop up the imps he summons before they reach the healer.

    King Gordok (North) & the tribute run. If you kill every North boss, you fight the King with his shaman Cho'Rush helping. But if you SKIP the other bosses (with tricks: an ogre suit costume, freezing one boss in a trap), the King dies alone and the surviving bosses PAY YOU TRIBUTE — a chest of extra loot. The tribute run is the connoisseur's clear. ⚔️ Damage: Cho'Rush the observer must SURVIVE — there is no tribute chest if he dies. 💚 Healer: Gordok's last stretch is the healing check.

    Also in the wings. East: Pusillin the imp (the chase IS the fight — he drops the Crescent Key for West/North), Hydrospawn (a water elemental in the pools), Lethtendris (a spider-riding satyr with an imp pet). West: Tendris Warpwood (a treant who roots and summons lashers), Magister Kalendris (a ghost mage), Tsu'zee (a banshee in the library halls). North: Guards Mol'dar, Fengus and Slip'kik and Stomper Kreeg (each a plain ogre brawl — and each one you SKIP, alive, pays tribute after the King dies), plus Captain Kromcrush, who fears the tank and calls guards — the tribute run's hardest decision. 💚 Healer: Zevrim (East) teleports a random player away and drains them — find and heal that player fast. ⚔️ Damage: Pusillin — don't spend cooldowns while he's running; he stops, then he dies. Immol'thar: kill the Eyes he summons.

    Watch out. Ogre packs in North hit like trucks and the patrols are dense. West is caster-heavy — interrupts matter again.

    Where. Also inside Blackrock Mountain, above Blackrock Depths — the orc and dragonkin fortress. It has two halves: Lower (5 players) and Upper (a 10-player dungeon in the original design).

    Levels & size. 55–60. Lower is a full 5-player dungeon of about 1.5 hours; Upper is the pre-raid gearing step.

    Before you go. Upper's door is sealed: the Seal of Ascension ring (a quest chain from Lower's bosses) is the key — one person in the group must have it. Lower also holds the famous 'Leeroy' room (the rookery) — yes, that one. It's real, it's here, the whelps are exactly as advertised.

    The route. Lower: up the orc halls in a spiral — Omokk's ogres, Vosh'gajin's trolls, Voone, the spider caves (Smolderweb), Urok's ogre mound (a summoning event), Zigris, the beast's pens (Halycon and Gizrul), and Wyrmthalak at the top. Upper (10-player): through the rookery (the egg room — step carefully, or don't, and meet the legend), past Rend Blackhand's arena, to Drakkisath's chamber.

    Overlord Wyrmthalak (Lower). The final boss of Lower, a dragonspawn commander who calls ogre and dragonkin guards at health thresholds. Kill the guards as they come.

    General Drakkisath (Upper). The final boss of Upper and of the pre-raid game. He periodically sets one player ablaze (conflagration) — that player must run AWAY from the group until it passes. His two dragonspawn guards die first. Killing him is also part of the Onyxia raid attunement chain. ⚔️ Damage: his two guards die first. 🛡️ Tank: when he sets YOU on fire (Conflagration), you run — the group must survive without you until it burns out. 💚 Healer: the burning target needs distance first, heals second.

    Also in the Spire. Highlord Omokk (an ogre chief — knocks the tank around), Shadow Hunter Vosh'gajin (a troll caster with hexes), War Master Voone (an axe-throwing troll), Mother Smolderweb (her spiderlings swarm — area damage wins), Urok Doomhowl (summoned by placing the ogre head pike in his mound — an event, bring the pike), Quartermaster Zigris (a dragonspawn with bloodlusted guards), Halycon (a great worg) and her master Gizrul the Slavener in the beast pens. 🛡️ Tank: in the Rend arena event, gather each wave as it lands. ⚔️ Damage: burn the dragon Gyth when Rend dismounts to join the fight. 💚 Healer: Mother Smolderweb can petrify the tank solid — heal the group through the pause.

    Watch out. Dragonkin in the Spire resist fire and hit hard; the orc packs include healers (kill them first, always).

    Where. In Azshara, the ruined high elf zone on Kalimdor's north-east coast — one of the strongholds from Forever's new endgame set.

    Levels & size. 55–60, five players. One of the two new high-end dungeons.

    Before you go. Azshara is remote — fly to the nearest point and ride as a group. At this level your group should be in mostly dungeon-blue gear; if you're fresh 60s, Dire Maul and Lower Spire gear you up for here. Boss facts below are cross-checked from community clear guides (Mobalytics and others) — Icy Veins hasn't published a boss guide for this one yet.

    The route. The Hold opens into Blackmaw Grove, with three branch bosses you can take in any order: Withermaw (left branch, the Festering Grotto), Bonesnout (below the central bridge), and Impaan (up the right path to Defiled Peak). Then cross the bridge into the lower hold: the Unbound Spirit event, Elder Shaman Bloodmane, the Behemoth and Clawbert, and Varrox Bleakhoof at the end.

    Withermaw. A giant bear in the Festering Grotto. He summons Flesh Maggots mid-fight (the tank picks them up; they die off on their own) and FEARS the group — clear the trash around him first so nobody runs into a fresh pack. Fear Ward or a Tremor Totem makes this easy. 🛡️ Tank: hold him center-room, so his fear can't scatter the group into uncleared trash. ⚔️ Damage: his Flesh Maggots die off on their own — don't chase them.

The trick: he takes 25% LESS damage for every Legashi Ritualist still alive near him — kill all the ritualists before you pull him, or the fight takes forever. His close-range stomp stuns: step away while he casts it. ⚔️ Damage & 🛡️ Tank: nobody pulls until every Ritualist around him is dead — each one alive cuts the damage he takes by 25%. ⚔️ Damage: step away while he casts his stomp.

    Impaan the Imprisoner. A demon up on Defiled Peak. His casts must be interrupted, and he summons an imp that MUST die before Impaan himself can die — when the imp appears, everyone switches to it, then finishes him. ⚔️ Damage: his summoned imp dies the second it appears — Impaan cannot die while it lives. Assign his interrupts before the pull.

He channels three Profane Totems vulnerable one at a time — kill each totem as it opens, and defend him from the waves that come for him. When all three fall, the Spirit wakes — reportedly the easy part. ⚔️ Damage: kill each Profane Totem the moment the Elder makes it vulnerable, and defend him from the waves in between.

    Elder Shaman Bloodmane. He opens by summoning two spectral wolves — ignore them and burn the boss (cleave them down only if they're hurting). Interrupt his Chain Lightning. Clear his chamber before the pull. ⚔️ Damage: ignore his two spectral wolves and burn him (cleave them only if they're hurting). His Chain Lightning gets interrupted — assign it.

    Blackmaw Behemoth & Clawbert. A pair: Clawbert, a furbolg, constantly HEALS the Behemoth — kill Clawbert first or the fight never ends. When Clawbert dies the Behemoth enrages and starts healing itself, so the second half is a race. Interrupt the Behemoth's shout (it cuts your group's damage) and stay clear of its whirlwind. ⚔️ Damage: Clawbert dies FIRST — he heals the Behemoth. Interrupt the Behemoth's shout and stay clear of his whirlwind. 💚 Healer: once Clawbert dies the Behemoth enrages and heals himself — the second half is a race.

Move away from the controlled player, and never stand near old orbs: every new Crimson Wrest detonates all of them again. ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: Crimson Wrest — run AWAY from the mind-controlled player before they explode, and never stand near the red orbs left behind: every new Wrest detonates all of them again. ⚔️ Damage: interrupt his Fear and his Shadow Bolt volleys.

    Where. In the Eastern Plaguelands — the burning city where the plague began. It has two entrances and two halves: the 'Live' side (Scarlet Crusade humans) and the 'Undead' side (Scourge), meeting at the end.

    Shaper's Terrace — NEW in ForeverTap to open⌄

    Where. In the northern hills of Un'Goro Crater — a titan terrace, the highest of Forever's new dungeons and its level-60 capstone.

    Levels & size. 58–60, five players. The newest endgame dungeon in the game.

    Before you go. BRING POWER CRYSTALS — 15 of EACH color (red, blue, green, yellow): the dungeon's four panels eat them, and bringing them from your Un'Goro questing saves a lot of in-dungeon farming. One player must hold all 15 of a color to feed a panel. Fresh-60 gear from Dire Maul/Spire first. Boss facts below come from community clear guides (Warcraft Tavern and others) — coverage of this dungeon is still thin, and the card says so where it matters.

    The route. An open crater you can ride across — there's no single corridor. Scattered dinosaur bosses (Titanosaur, the Direhorn Matriarch, Thunderhoof, Venomridge, and the Tor'Rel & Izha'Rel pair) can be fought in any order. The spine of the dungeon is the four Archival Panels: feed each one 15 Power Crystals of its color (red, blue, green, yellow — bring them from your Un'Goro questing; ONE player must hold all 15) to summon Cinder, Snowtalon, Vitrix and Bolt. All four panels fed unlocks the Titan Panel at the central Pillar of Assimilation — and the final boss, Nanaya.

    Roles — the whole run. ⚔️ Damage — the crystals are your job too: one player carries all 15 of a color to its panel, and bringing them from Un'Goro saves a mid-run farm. 🛡️ Tank — everything here patrols or wanders: clear around a boss before engaging it, every time.

    Cinder (red panel). A fire raptor summoned by the Red Archival Panel, up the west mountain path. Clear the fire elementals around him first and the fight itself is simple; fire resistance (like a Paladin's aura) helps. ⚔️ Damage: clear the fire elementals around him first — then he's simple. 💚 Healer: fire resistance (like a Paladin's aura) softens the whole fight.

    Snowtalon (blue panel). An ice raptor in a cave behind a waterfall. Frost Nova plus Cone of Cold punish a bunched-up group — pull him OUT of the cave first, and the tank keeps him facing away from everyone else. 🛡️ Tank: pull him OUT of the cave and face him away from the group — Frost Nova plus Cone of Cold punish anyone bunched in front of him.

    Vitrix (green panel) — the dangerous one. Honest warning: early in Forever, Vitrix's Poison Shock could wipe a whole party in one hit (Blizzard reduced it on Nov 19 — how much safer he is now isn't settled). You do NOT have to kill him: feeding the Green Panel its crystals is what counts for finishing the dungeon. Attempt the kill geared and careful, or skip it proudly. ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: the kill is OPTIONAL — feeding his panel is all the dungeon requires. If you attempt him, cooldowns go up front: his Poison Shock is the one-shot risk.

    Bolt (yellow panel). A lightning boss by the tar pits. He casts Chain Lightning INSTANTLY on random players — it can't be interrupted, so spread out (ranged and healers especially) so it can't jump between you, and heal through it. ⚔️ Damage & 💚 Healer: spread out — his Chain Lightning is instant and cannot be interrupted, so distance is the only defense.

    Titanosaur. The devilsaur patrolling the main crater road. He fears the WHOLE party — so clear every pack around his patrol path before engaging, or the fear turns into three fights at once. A Tremor Totem makes it comfortable. 🛡️ Tank: engage only after everything along his patrol path is dead — his party-wide fear sends everyone running into whatever is left.

    Tor'Rel & Izha'Rel. A paired raptor patrol on the northern path. Tor'Rel deals fire damage; Izha'Rel deals ice and wears Frost Armor, which punishes anyone hitting her in melee. Kill Tor'Rel first, then her. When they get low they summon Ravasaur Stalker adds — the tank must be ready to grab them. ⚔️ Damage: Tor'Rel (fire) dies first; Izha'Rel's Frost Armor punishes melee hitting her. 🛡️ Tank: be ready to grab the Ravasaur Stalker adds when the pair gets low.

    Direhorn Matriarch. A great horned dinosaur on the east side. Never stand directly behind her (tail swipe) — she also charges random players, so stay loosely grouped at her sides. Clear her surroundings first, like everything here. 🛡️ Tank: keep her side to the group — her tail swipe and her charges punish clustering behind her.

    Thunderhoof. A thunder lizard on the southern rim. His lightning attacks can and should be interrupted — assign one person to it. If interrupts are thin, nature-resistance gear is the backup plan. ⚔️ Damage: his lightning casts can and should be interrupted — assign one person to it before the pull.

    Venomridge. A dinosaur in the tar pits who periodically poisons the ENTIRE party. A Shaman's Poison Cleansing Totem makes this fight nearly free; without one, cure poisons fast or heal through them. 💚 Healer: he poisons the ENTIRE party on a timer — a Shaman's Poison Cleansing Totem makes it nearly free; otherwise, dispel triage is the fight.

    Nanaya (final boss). A giant in titan armor at the Pillar of Assimilation, summoned from the Titan Panel once all four color panels are fed. She 'Energizes' from the crystal formations around the platform, switching her damage type and gaining matching spells (blue crystals: Blizzard; yellow: Chain Lightning), and she sometimes stuns the group around her. Spectacular — but with standard positioning, reportedly not the hardest thing in here.     November 10, 2026 — the Enhancement reworkTap to open⌄

    🌀 Shaman — Enhancement, reworked

    • Stormstrike: damage reduced to 100% weapon damage (was 150%), cooldown increased to 8 seconds (was 6).

    • Mental Dexterity: Intellect converts to Attack Power at 33/66/100% per rank (was 50/100/150%).

    • Maelstrom Weapon: now procs 6–10 times per minute depending on rank (was 20), but every stack gives the full 20% cast-time and mana reduction (was 4% per rank, stacking). Its tooltip now shows your real proc chance for your weapon's speed.

    • Weapon imbues and weapon totems stack now — Windfury Weapon and Windfury Totem can both be active, and an attack can trigger both.

    • Blizzard's reasoning, in short: beta changes had pushed Enhancement toward spell-power gear and away from Windfury. They reversed that now, before raids open, so nobody wins raid gear that's about to drop in value.
